# Interview Question Bank & Hiring Guidance (U.S. Compliance)


This section provides a reusable bank of structured, job-related interview questions and hiring guidance for the Organization. It is intended for use by staff involved in recruiting, interviewing, and selecting employees or contractors. All questions must be applied in a consistent, nondiscriminatory manner and remain focused on the applicant's ability to perform the essential functions of the role, with or without reasonable accommodation, in accordance with applicable U.S. federal, state, and local laws.

### 12.1 General Behavioral & Situational Performance Questions

• Please walk us through a recent project or responsibility that is most similar to this role. What were your specific responsibilities, and what was the outcome?  
• Tell us about a time you had to manage multiple priorities or deadlines at once. How did you organize your work, and what was the result?  
• Describe a situation where you received critical feedback about your work. What was the feedback, and how did you respond?  
• Give an example of a time you had to solve a difficult problem with incomplete information. What steps did you take, and what did you learn?  
• Tell us about a time you worked as part of a team to accomplish a goal. What was your role, and how did you handle any disagreements or challenges?  
• Describe a situation where you had to adapt quickly to a change in direction, policy, or leadership priorities. How did you handle it?

### 12.2 Accessibility, Communication & Inclusive Practice

• the Organization serves Deaf, hard-of-hearing, and hearing children and families. Please describe your experience working with Deaf or hard-of-hearing people, Deaf communities, or Deaf-led organizations, if any. What did you learn from those experiences?  
• How do you ensure that information is communicated in an accessible way to people with a variety of communication preferences (e.g., American Sign Language, signed systems, spoken English, captioning, visual supports)? Please share a specific example.  
• Describe a time when you needed to adjust your communication approach to be more inclusive or effective for a Deaf or hard-of-hearing colleague, client, or partner. What did you do, and what was the impact?  
• This role requires working respectfully within Deaf culture and with diverse language choices. Can you share an example of how you have demonstrated cultural humility or responsiveness in your work?  
• (If ASL or other language is an essential function of the role): This position requires proficiency in [American Sign Language / other specified language] to perform essential duties. Please describe your professional experience using [ASL/other] in work or volunteer settings and how you currently use or maintain those skills.

• Scenario: You are planning a family-facing event for the Organization. Some families prefer ASL, some prefer spoken English with interpreters or captioning, and some are new to Deaf culture. How would you plan communication access for this event, and how would you explain your approach to families who have different preferences?

### 12.3 Equity, Inclusion & Culturally Responsive Service Delivery

• the Organization serves families from diverse cultural, linguistic, and socioeconomic backgrounds. Please share an example of how you have adapted a program, service, or communication to better meet the needs of a diverse audience.  
• Describe a time you noticed that a process, program, or policy was not equitable or was unintentionally excluding some people. What did you do?  
• Tell us about a situation where you worked with individuals whose backgrounds or perspectives differed significantly from your own. How did you build trust and understanding?  
• How do you incorporate feedback from families or community members — especially those who may feel marginalized — into your work?

### 12.4 Remote Work, Professional Boundaries & Confidentiality

• Many the Organization roles involve remote or hybrid work. Please describe how you organize your workday when working remotely to meet deadlines and stay responsive to colleagues and stakeholders.  
• Describe a time you handled confidential or sensitive information (for example, about families, donors, or staff). How did you make sure it was protected?  
• Scenario: You receive an email with what appears to be confidential family information sent to you in error. What steps would you take?  
• Scenario: During a virtual meeting from your home or remote location, you realize others nearby might overhear the conversation. How would you address this in the moment and for future meetings?

### 12.5 Role-Specific Scenarios & Skills Validation

• Program / Service Delivery Roles:  
  • Scenario: You are responsible for coordinating a virtual workshop for families across time zones. Please walk us through how you would plan, promote, deliver, and follow up on this workshop.  
  • What tools or platforms have you used to deliver online events or trainings, and how do you ensure accessibility (e.g., interpreters, captioning, visual materials)?  
• Administrative / Operations Roles:  
  • Describe your experience with managing calendars, email communications, and documentation for a busy team or organization. What systems or tools have you found most effective?  
  • Scenario: You notice discrepancies between an invoice and a contract. How would you investigate and address the issue?  
• Leadership / Management Roles:  
  • Tell us about a time you led a team through a period of change or limited resources. How did you set priorities, communicate with the team, and monitor results?  
  • Scenario: Two team members have an ongoing conflict that is affecting collaboration. How would you address this as their supervisor?  
• Technical / Specialized Skills (to be tailored to the position):  
  • This position requires proficiency in [specific software, platform, or technical skill]. Please describe your experience using [tool/skill] and a recent project where you relied on it.  
  • How do you keep your [specified] skills current, and how have you applied them in a nonprofit or mission-driven context, if at all?

### 12.6 Questions to Avoid (U.S. Legal & the Organization Policy)

Interviewers must not ask questions (in any form) that directly or indirectly seek information about any of the following, except where an applicable law expressly requires or permits it and only after consulting with the Executive Director and legal counsel as needed:

• Protected classes and personal characteristics  
  • Age or date of birth (other than confirming legal minimum working age where required).  
  • Race, color, national origin, ancestry, or ethnicity.  
  • Sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression.  
  • Religion, religious practices, or beliefs.  
  • Citizenship or immigration status beyond what is required for lawful work authorization (e.g., no questions about country of origin or how citizenship was obtained).  
  • Marital status, pregnancy, reproductive health decisions, or plans to marry or have children.  
  • Military or veteran status, except as allowed by law for affirmative outreach or where directly job-related.

• Medical, disability, and health information  
  • Questions about disabilities, medical conditions, diagnoses, past or current treatment, medications, workers' compensation history, or mental health history.  
  • Questions about prior sick leave usage or medical absences.  
  • Questions about whether the applicant “is disabled,” “has any health issues,” or similar.  
  • Instead of asking about medical or disability status, interviewers may describe the essential functions of the job (including any physical, communication, or scheduling requirements) and ask: “Are you able to perform these essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation?”

• Family status, caregiving, and personal life  
  • Whether an applicant is married, has children, is pregnant, or plans to become pregnant.  
  • Childcare arrangements, elder care responsibilities, or other caregiving details.  
  • Living situation, housing stability, or with whom the applicant lives.  
  • Instead of asking about these topics, interviewers may ask neutral, job-related questions about availability and scheduling needs, such as: “This role may require occasional evening or weekend work with advance notice. Are you able to meet that requirement?”

### 12.7 Handling Requests for Interview or Job Accommodations

The Organization is committed to providing reasonable accommodations to qualified applicants and staff with disabilities, consistent with applicable law and the Organization policy.

• If an applicant requests an accommodation for the interview process (for example, interpreters, captioning, accessible documents, alternative formats, or schedule adjustments):  
  • Thank the applicant for sharing the request.  
  • Limit questions to understanding what accommodation is needed for the interview or for performing essential job functions; do not ask for medical diagnoses or details.  
  • Coordinate promptly with the Executive Director (and, if applicable, HR or designated staff) to determine what reasonable accommodations the Organization can provide.  
  • Confirm the accommodations in writing (e.g., email), including any logistics the applicant needs to know.  
• If an applicant asks whether the Organization can provide accommodations on the job:  
  • Describe, at a high level, the Organization's commitment to reasonable accommodation and, if available, applicable policies.  
  • Focus on the essential functions of the job and how accommodations can support performance of those functions, rather than on the nature of the applicant's disability or medical condition.  
• Documentation and escalation:  
  • Interviewers should document accommodation requests and the steps taken to respond, and store this information in accordance with the Organization confidentiality practices and any HR guidance.  
  • If there is uncertainty about whether a requested accommodation is reasonable, or about how to respond, interviewers must escalate promptly to the Executive Director, who may consult with legal counsel or HR professionals as needed.

### 12.8 Accommodations Handling Checklist (Interviews & Hiring)

For each accommodations request in the interview or hiring process, interviewers should:  
• Receive the request respectfully  
• Listen and thank the applicant for raising the need.  
• Clarify needs without probing disability details  
• Ask only what is necessary to understand the accommodation for the interview or essential job functions.  
• Coordinate internally  
• Contact the Executive Director (and, if applicable, HR or designated staff) promptly to determine reasonable accommodations.  
• Confirm in writing  
• Send a brief written confirmation (e.g., email) summarizing the agreed accommodations and logistics.  
• Preserve confidentiality  
• Store any notes or documentation consistent with the Organization confidentiality practices and HR guidance.  
• Escalate when unsure  
• If unsure whether a requested accommodation is reasonable, or how to respond, escalate promptly to the Executive Director for further guidance (including legal/HR consultation as needed).
