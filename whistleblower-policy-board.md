**3.6 Whistleblower Policy for Board Members**

| Reviewed/Revised: April 2026                                      | Effective Date: July 1, 2026 |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Authorized By: the Organization Board, July 1, 2026 |                              |

1.  **Background and Objectives**

    1.  The [Organization Name] Code of Conduct requires directors, officers, other volunteers, and employees to observe high standards of business and personal ethics in the conduct of their duties and responsibilities. Board members, in particular, must practice honesty and integrity in fulfilling their fiduciary and oversight responsibilities and comply with all applicable laws and regulations, the Organization’s bylaws, and Board policies, including this Whistleblower Policy for Board Members.

    2.  The objectives of this Whistleblower Policy are to establish procedures for Board members and others to:

        1.  Submit concerns regarding suspected violations of law; questionable, unethical, or improper conduct; violations of the Organization’s bylaws, policies (including the Code of Conduct and Conflict of Interest Policy), or contractual obligations; and questionable accounting, internal control, or auditing matters, by directors, officers, volunteers, contractors, employees, and other stakeholders of the organization, on a confidential and, if chosen, anonymous basis;

        2.  Ensure the receipt, documentation, retention, and appropriate treatment of complaints received by the organization regarding suspected violations, including without limitation accounting, internal controls, financial reporting, misuse of organizational resources, harassment or discrimination, and other governance or ethical concerns;

        3.  Protect directors, officers, volunteers, contractors, employees, and other persons who report concerns or participate in investigations in good faith from retaliatory actions.

2.  **Reporting Responsibility**

    1.  Each director, officer, volunteer, contractor, and employee of the [Organization Name] has an obligation to report, in accordance with this Whistleblower Policy, any concern the individual reasonably believes may involve misconduct by the Organization, its directors, officers, employees, volunteers, or contractors. Board members have a heightened obligation, consistent with their fiduciary duties, to report and address such concerns.

        1.  (a) any suspected violation of federal, state, or local law or regulation,

        2.  (b) questionable or improper accounting, internal control, financial reporting, or auditing matters, and

        3.  (c) violations and suspected violations of the Code of Conduct, Conflict of Interest Policy, this Whistleblower Policy, the Organization’s bylaws, or other material organizational policies, as well as other serious ethical or governance concerns (hereinafter collectively referred to as Concerns).

3.  **Authority of Executive Director and Board**

    1.  Except as provided in Section 3.3, all reported Concerns submitted under this policy will be forwarded promptly to the Executive Director and the President of the Board of Directors in accordance with the procedures set forth herein.

    2.  The Executive Director will, under the general oversight of the President of the Board of Directors or the Board-designated compliance lead, investigate or cause to be investigated all reported Concerns and make appropriate recommendations regarding findings and corrective or disciplinary action.

3.3. If a Concern involves, or reasonably appears to involve, the Executive Director, the President of the Board of Directors, any officer of the Board, or any member of the Board of Directors, the Concern shall be reported and forwarded directly to the Vice President of the Board of Directors or to such other Board officer or committee (such as the Governance or Audit/Finance Committee) as the Board may designate for this purpose. In such cases, the implicated individual(s) shall be recused from all review, deliberation, and decision-making related to the Concern and any resulting investigation or action.

3.4. The Executive Director, the President of the Board of Directors, or the Board-designated committee, as applicable, has the authority, with notice to the Board President or Vice President (as appropriate), to retain outside legal counsel, accountants, human resources professionals, private investigators, or any other resources deemed necessary to conduct a full and fair investigation and to advise the Organization regarding appropriate corrective, remedial, or disciplinary action.

4.  **No Retaliation**

    1.  This Whistleblower Policy is intended to encourage and enable directors, officers, volunteers, contractors, employees, and other stakeholders to raise Concerns within the Organization for investigation and appropriate action without fear of retaliation. The Organization will not tolerate retaliation against any individual who, in good faith, reports a Concern, cooperates with an investigation, or participates in a related proceeding.

    2.  With this goal in mind, no director, officer, volunteer, contractor, or employee who, in good faith, reports a Concern or participates in an investigation shall be subject to retaliation, including but not limited to adverse employment consequences, removal from a volunteer or Board role, intimidation, threats, harassment, or other actions that would discourage good-faith reporting.

    3.  Moreover, any director, officer, volunteer, contractor, or employee who retaliates against someone who has reported a Concern or participated in an investigation in good faith is subject to discipline, up to and including removal from the Board, dismissal from a volunteer position, termination of employment, or termination of a contractual relationship, as applicable. Retaliation may also give rise to other actions, including civil or criminal liability.

Protection from retaliation applies even if a reported Concern is not substantiated, provided the report was made in good faith and on a reasonable belief that the information disclosed was accurate at the time of reporting.

5.  **Reporting Concerns**

    1.  Employees, Contractors, Directors, and Volunteers

        1.  Employees, contractors, directors, and volunteers should submit Concerns in writing (including by letter or secure electronic communication) to the Executive Director or to the email or other reporting mechanism designated by the Organization for this purpose.

        2.  If the individual is uncomfortable speaking with the Executive Director, or if the Executive Director is a subject of the Concern, the individual should report the Concern directly to the President of the Board of Directors, or to the Vice President or Board-designated committee if the Concern involves both the Executive Director and the President of the Board of Directors.

        3.  If the Concern is reported verbally, the Executive Director, the President of the Board of Directors, or the applicable Board officer or committee designee shall promptly reduce the Concern to writing, including the date, nature of the Concern, and the identity of the reporter (if known), and, where feasible, confirm the written summary with the reporter.

        4.  Anonymous Concerns should be submitted in writing and may be sent directly to the Executive Director, to the President or Vice President of the Board of Directors, or through any anonymous reporting mechanism that the Organization may establish from time to time (such as a dedicated email address, web form, or telephone line).

    2.  The Executive Director, the President of the Board of Directors, or the Board-designated committee, as applicable under this policy, shall address all reported Concerns.

        1.  The responsible party under Section 5.2 will use reasonable efforts to notify the sender and acknowledge receipt of the Concern within thirty business days, if the identity and contact information of the sender are known and such acknowledgment is consistent with maintaining the integrity and confidentiality of the investigation.

        2.  It will not be possible to acknowledge receipt of anonymously submitted Concerns. The absence of an acknowledgment does not mean that the Concern has not been received or will not be reviewed.

        3.  All reports will be promptly and impartially reviewed and, where appropriate, investigated by the Executive Director or the applicable Board officer or committee, with the assistance of legal counsel or other advisors as needed, and appropriate corrective, remedial, or disciplinary action will be recommended if warranted by the investigation.

        4.  Action taken must include a documented conclusion and, where appropriate and consistent with confidentiality and privacy considerations, follow-up with the complainant for closure of the Concern. Significant Concerns and resulting actions, particularly those involving directors or officers, shall be reported to the full Board (with any implicated individuals recused) in a timely manner.

        5.  Responses to the complainant of an anonymous Concern may not be possible; however, where feasible and appropriate, the Organization may provide general information about resolution of such Concerns through the same anonymous reporting mechanism or other means that do not compromise confidentiality.

        6.  The Executive Director, the President of the Board of Directors, or the Board-designated committee, as applicable, has the authority to retain outside legal counsel, accountants, human resources professionals, private investigators, or any other resources deemed necessary to conduct a full and complete investigation of the allegations and to advise the Organization and the Board regarding appropriate responses and corrective actions.

The Organization will maintain records of all Concerns and related investigations, including the initial report (if in writing), any written summaries of verbal reports, investigation plans and notes, findings, and documentation of any corrective or disciplinary action taken. These records shall be retained in accordance with the Organization’s record retention and destruction policy and applicable law and, in any event, for not less than seven (7) years from the date the Concern is resolved, unless legal counsel advises otherwise.

6.  **Acting in Good Faith**

    1.  Anyone reporting a Concern must act in good faith and have reasonable grounds and preferably evidence for believing the information disclosed indicates an improper accounting or auditing practice, or a violation of the Code or the law.

    2.  The act of making allegations that prove to be unsubstantiated and that also prove to have been made maliciously, recklessly, or with the foreknowledge that the allegations are false will be viewed as a serious disciplinary offense and may result in discipline, up to and including removal from the Board, dismissal from a volunteer position, termination of employment, or termination of a contractual relationship, as applicable. Such conduct may also give rise to other actions, including civil lawsuits or criminal penalties.

7.  **Confidentiality**

    1.  Reports of Concerns, and investigations pertaining thereto, shall be kept confidential to the extent reasonably possible, consistent with the need to conduct an adequate and fair investigation; comply with applicable laws, regulations, and legal process; protect the safety and rights of individuals; and implement appropriate corrective action. This obligation applies to staff, Board members, officers, and any advisors involved in receiving, investigating, or addressing a Concern.

    2.  Disclosure of Concerns, or of information learned in the course of an investigation, to individuals not involved in the investigation or in decision-making about the resulting actions will be viewed as a serious disciplinary offense and may result in discipline, up to and including removal from the Board, termination of employment, dismissal from a volunteer position, or termination of a contractual relationship, as applicable. Limited disclosures may be made to those with a legitimate need to know in order to investigate the Concern, implement corrective action, or comply with legal or regulatory requirements. Unauthorized disclosures may also give rise to other actions, including civil lawsuits.

The Organization may, and in appropriate cases should, consult with legal counsel regarding the handling of Concerns, including to preserve applicable privileges, ensure compliance with legal obligations, and determine whether any reports to governmental or regulatory authorities are required or advisable.

# Board Oversight Responsibilities

The Board of Directors retains ultimate responsibility for oversight of this Whistleblower Policy and of the Organization's response to Concerns. The Board may delegate primary oversight to a standing committee (such as the Governance or Audit/Finance Committee), but such delegation does not relieve the Board of its overall fiduciary duties.

At least annually, and more frequently as needed, the Executive Director and/or the Board-designated committee shall provide the Board with a summary of Concerns raised under this policy and their resolution, in a form that protects confidentiality and does not disclose personally identifiable or sensitive information beyond what is reasonably necessary for oversight.

The Board shall ensure that the Organization allocates appropriate resources to implement this policy, including training for Board members and staff, the maintenance of secure reporting channels, and access to legal and other professional advisors as needed.

This Whistleblower Policy shall be reviewed by the Board, or its designee, no less than every three (3) years, or more frequently as needed, and updated as appropriate to reflect changes in law, regulation, best practices, or the Organization's organizational structure.

# Annual Acknowledgment by Board Members

Upon joining the Board and at least annually thereafter, each member of the Board of Directors shall receive a copy of this Whistleblower Policy and shall sign a written acknowledgment, in a form approved by the Board or its designee, that the Board member has received, read, and understands the policy; agrees to comply with it; and understands the duty to report Concerns and the protections against retaliation provided herein.

Signed acknowledgments shall be maintained with the Organization's corporate records in accordance with the Organization's record retention and destruction policy.
