# Staff Code of Conduct & Executive Director Scope (Staff Guide)

Purpose: This guide outlines the Organization staff conduct expectations, the Executive Director’s decision-making authority, and legally compliant hiring and interview practices to support consistent, mission-aligned, and lawful operations.

## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Applicability

This guide serves as the Organization’s Staff Code of Conduct and explains how decision-making, expectations, and escalation work at the [Organization Name]. It is written for the Organization employees, contractors, interns, and volunteers acting in a staff capacity (“staff”), and includes a legally compliant interview question bank for use across the Organization roles.

It is intended to:

- Clarify the Executive Director’s scope of authority in day-to-day operations and staff oversight.
- Distinguish decisions the ED can make independently from those requiring approval by:
- Officers (President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer),
- The Executive Committee, or
- The full Board of Directors.
- Set clear thresholds for budget, spending, and contracts.
- Outline hiring and personnel approval rules (including any referenced in the ED employment agreement).
- Define reporting lines and communication expectations.
- Establish confidentiality expectations.
- Provide practical escalation paths and timelines for HR, safety, finance, legal, and reputational issues.
- Offer simple templates staff can use when escalating issues.

This guide is **interpretive and operational**. If there is any conflict between this guide and the the Organization Bylaws or a signed agreement approved by the Board (including the ED Employment Agreement), the Bylaws and Board-approved agreements control.

## 2. Governance Context

### 2.1 Board of Directors

- Governs the affairs and property of the Organization.
- Hires (and may remove) the Executive Director.
- Approves:
- Mission, strategic direction, and major policy positions.
- Annual operating and capital budgets.
- Major contracts and commitments above certain thresholds.
- Creation and elimination of senior leadership positions.
- Key governance policies (e.g., conflict-of-interest, nondiscrimination).

### 2.2 Officers and Executive Committee

- **Officers**: President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer (and Past President as a Board role).
- **Executive Committee** (per the Bylaws): President, Vice President, Past President, Secretary, Treasurer, and Executive Director.
- The Executive Committee:
- Acts between Board meetings to carry out customary business and implement established Board policies and mandates.
- **Cannot** amend Bylaws or reverse Board actions.
- Must have its actions ratified at the next Board meeting.

### 2.3 Executive Director

- Hired by and serves at the discretion of the Board.
- Reports to the President of the Board (and, as specified in the ED Employment Agreement, may communicate directly with specific officers on defined topics).
- Serves as an ex officio, non-voting member of the Board and Board committees (unless otherwise stated).
- Holds overall strategic and operational responsibility for the Organization staff, programs, financial and administrative operations, fundraising, and communications, as set out in:
- the Organization Bylaws (Executive Director provisions), and
- The ED Employment Agreement (services and Statement of Work).

## 4. Financial Authority: Budget, Spending & Contracts

All financial decisions must comply with:

- The Board-approved annual budget.
- Applicable federal, state, and local laws.
- the Organization’s financial policies and internal controls.

### 4.1 General Principles

1. The ED **manages operations within the Board-approved budget**, including reallocations within reasonable limits.
2. Spending and contracts must be:
- Within budgeted purposes,
- Within authority thresholds, and
- Properly documented and approved before commitment.
3. No staff member (including the ED) may:
- Personally benefit from the Organization transactions beyond approved compensation and expense reimbursement.
- Bind the Organization outside their authority limits.

### 4.2 Spending Authority Within Approved Budget

Unless superseded by a Board resolution or formal financial policy, the following operational thresholds apply to **individual commitments** (not annual totals):

- Up to \$5,000 per transaction
- **Authority**: ED may approve and sign, if:
- The expense is within the Board-approved budget and line of business.
- Standard procurement practices are followed (e.g., at least one competitive quote when practical).
- **Notification**: Routine; no pre-notice required. Finance records must be updated.
- \$5,001 – \$15,000 per transaction
- **Authority**: ED may approve, with:
- **Pre-clearance from the Treasurer or President** (email is sufficient) for non-routine or new vendors.
- At least **two quotes** (unless sole-source is justified in writing).
- **Notification**: Treasurer receives copy of agreement/invoice; summary provided in monthly financial report to the Board.
- Above \$15,000 per transaction
- **Authority**: Requires **Executive Committee or Board** approval before commitment.
- ED prepares a short memo including:
- Description of the commitment.
- Total cost and term.
- Budget line(s) and funding source.
- Rationale and alternatives considered.
- **Signature**: President (or designee) signs on behalf of the Organization after approval.

### 4.3 Spending Outside Approved Budget (Variances)

- Minor variances up to \$2,500 in aggregate per budget line
- ED may reallocate within the same functional area (e.g., programs, communications) if total annual budget is not exceeded more than 5% for that line.
- Requires documentation and explanation in the next monthly financial report.
- Moderate variances (\$2,501 – \$10,000) or >5% of line-item
- ED drafts a brief variance request (see template §11.3).
- Requires **prior written approval** from Treasurer and President.
- Significant variances (above \$10,000 or >10% of total approved budget)
- Requires **Board approval** (or Executive Committee approval with subsequent Board ratification).
- ED provides a variance memo with updated projections.

### 4.4 Contracting Authority

All contracts must:

- Name “[Organization Name]” as the contracting party.
- Be reviewed for legal and financial risk appropriate to their size and complexity.
- Be signed only by those with explicit authority.

**Contract thresholds:**

- Service, consulting, or vendor contracts up to \$10,000 total value (and < 1-year term)
- ED may negotiate and sign, provided:
- The contract is within the approved budget.
- Standard terms are used where available (e.g., the Organization’s standard vendor or contractor agreement).
- ED must ensure appropriate documentation (e.g., W-9, insurance certificates if required).
- \$10,001 – \$25,000 or multi-year contracts (up to 3 years)
- ED may negotiate the terms.
- Signature requires **President** (or Board designee) after:
- Review by Treasurer for affordability and consistency with budget.
- Consultation with legal counsel as appropriate.
- Any contract:
- Over **\$25,000 total value**,
- With a term longer than 3 years,
- That creates long-term liabilities (e.g., leases, technology platforms with auto-renewal and high penalties),
- Or that involves material reputational or legal risk
- **Requires Board approval** prior to signing.

## 5. Hiring, Personnel & Approval Requirements

The ED Employment Agreement  and Board-approved policies govern the ED’s authority over hiring and personnel. This section translates those principles into a clear staff-facing guide.

### 5.1 General HR Principles

- the Organization is an equal opportunity employer and follows applicable nondiscrimination laws.
- Hiring and personnel decisions must support the Organization’s mission and be financially sustainable.
- Documentation of recruitment, hiring, performance, and termination decisions is required.

### 5.2 ED Authority for Hiring and Staffing

Subject to the ED employment agreement and Board-approved staffing plans:

- The ED may:
- Recruit, select, and hire staff and contractors for Board-approved positions within approved budget and compensation bands.
- Supervise, evaluate, and direct the work of staff and contractors.
- Make decisions on promotions, performance improvement plans, and routine disciplinary actions.
- Engage short-term consultants or contractors within contracting thresholds (see §4.4).
- The ED must obtain prior approval from the President (and, where relevant, Treasurer) for:
- Creating any **new staff or contractor role** not explicitly included in the Board-approved budget or authorized staffing plan.
- Offering **compensation or benefits outside approved ranges** or that materially increase total personnel costs.
- Hiring or changing the terms of **any direct report who would be considered “senior leadership”** (e.g., Director-level or equivalent, or any position that reports directly to the ED and has broad organizational authority).
- Any hiring decision that could reasonably be expected to trigger **legal, financial, or reputational risk**, including:
- Candidates with known conflicts of interest.
- Hiring that may create the appearance of favoritism or nepotism.

### 5.3 Terminations and Serious Personnel Actions

- The ED may independently:
- Issue written warnings, performance improvement plans, and other routine performance management communications.
- Terminate employment or contractor relationships for:
- Poor performance documented over time; or
- Misconduct, after appropriate fact-finding,
- **Except** as limited below.
- The ED must consult and secure approval from the President (and legal counsel, when needed) before:
- Terminating a **senior leadership** or direct-report role.
- Implementing a **reduction in force** or position elimination.
- Offering or agreeing to **severance payments**, waivers, or settlement agreements.
- Implementing any disciplinary or termination decision involving:
- Allegations of discrimination, harassment, retaliation, or safety violations.
- Any circumstance where legal claims or complaints are likely.

### 5.4 Reporting Lines (Staff & Contractors)

- **Executive Director**
- Reports to: President of the Board (with specific direct communication channels to the Treasurer and Secretary for finance and records-related matters, per the ED Agreement).
- **Staff and Contractors**
- All staff and contractors ultimately report to the ED through their designated supervisor.
- Staff should **not** bypass their supervisor or the ED to give instructions to other staff on organizational matters, unless:
- There is an urgent safety or legal concern and the ED is unavailable, and
- They are acting at the explicit direction of the President or designated officer.

## 6. Communications, Escalation Path & Media Approvals

The Organization’s public voice and internal communication paths are critical to its mission, governance, and reputation. The ED has substantial responsibility but operates within clear escalation pathways and guardrails.

### 6.0 Communication & Escalation Path (Staff Guide)

### 6.1 Routine Communications – ED Authority

The **ED may independently:**

- Approve and disseminate:
- Routine announcements (events, programs, newsletters, updates to families).
- Website and social media content that:
- Reflects approved positions, programs, and materials.
- Does not represent new or controversial policy stances.
- Serve as the Organization’s primary spokesperson for:
- Day-to-day operations.
- Programmatic updates.
- General fundraising and donor relations, following any donor engagement protocols established by the Board.

### 6.2 Communications Requiring Officer or Board Approval

The ED must **consult with and obtain approval** from the **President** (and, where time allows, the Executive Committee) before issuing statements or communications that:

- Involve **public policy, legislation, or regulatory advocacy** that may be politically sensitive or beyond existing Board-approved positions.
- Comment on **legal matters**, including:
- Active or threatened litigation,
- Complaints filed with regulatory agencies,
- Any statement that could be interpreted as an admission or concession.
- Respond to **crisis or reputational issues**, such as:
- Alleged harm to children or families.
- Safety incidents at the Organization-sponsored events.
- Public criticism of the Organization’s programs, staff, or partners that has or may attract media attention.
- Announce **major strategic or financial developments**, including:
- Large grants or partnerships that change the Organization’s scope.
- Program closures or major restructuring.
- Changes in ED or senior leadership.

**When in doubt, staff should:**

1. Alert the ED immediately.
2. The ED will then determine if and how to involve the President or Executive Committee.

## 7. Confidentiality Expectations

### 7.1 General Duty of Confidentiality

All staff and contractors must:

- Protect the Organization’s **confidential information**, including:
- Donor information and giving history.
- Staff and volunteer personnel information.
- Financial data not publicly disclosed.
- Unreleased program plans and strategies.
- Board materials and non-public Board discussions.
- Only use confidential information for legitimate the Organization purposes.
- Store and transmit information securely, following the Organization’s data protection practices.

The ED has heightened obligations under the Employment Agreement to maintain confidentiality of the Organization’s confidential and proprietary information.

### 7.2 Handling Sensitive Information

- **Personnel and HR Matters**
- May be shared only with:
- The ED,
- Relevant supervisors,
- HR/consultants engaged by the Organization,
- Officers and Board members when needed for governance purposes.
- **Donor and Financial Information**
- Accessed only on a “need-to-know” basis.
- Shared externally only with appropriate anonymization or consent.
- **Board and Committee Deliberations**
- Minutes, draft documents, and Board correspondence are confidential unless explicitly approved for release.
- The ED may share Board decisions with staff once the President (or Board) confirms they may be communicated.

### 7.3 Suspected Breaches

If a staff member suspects a confidentiality breach:

1. **Notify the ED immediately**, providing:
- What was disclosed.
- To whom and when.
- Any available evidence.
2. If the ED is implicated or unavailable:
- Notify the **President of the Board** directly.
3. Do **not** attempt to cover up or “fix” the problem by deleting records or altering documents without guidance.

## 8. Issue Escalation Framework

This section provides practical guidance on when and how staff should escalate issues to the ED, officers, or the Board.

### 8.1 Escalation Principles

- **Safety and legal obligations come first.** In emergencies, act to protect people and then notify leadership.
- **When in doubt, escalate.** It is better to over-communicate potential risk than to miss it.
- **Escalation goes up the chain:**
- Staff → Supervisor → ED → President/Officers → Executive Committee/Board.
- **Document key facts and steps taken.** Use the templates in §11.

### 8.2 Human Resources (HR) & Personnel Issues

**Examples:**

- Harassment, discrimination, or bullying.
- Conflicts between staff.
- Concerns about performance, conduct, or attendance.
- Alleged breaches of policy (e.g., misuse of the Organization resources).

**Staff responsibilities:**

- Report concerns to:
- Their direct supervisor, or
- The ED, if the supervisor is involved or unavailable, or
- The President of the Board if the concern involves the ED.

**Timelines:**

- **Serious allegations** (e.g., harassment, discrimination, retaliation):
- Report to ED (or President if ED is implicated) **within 24 hours**.
- **Other HR issues**:
- Report within **3 business days** of becoming aware of the issue.

**ED responsibilities:**

- **Serious allegations**:
- Begin fact-finding or engage HR/legal support **within 2 business days**.
- Inform the President **within 24 hours** of learning of the issue.
- **Less serious issues**:
- Address through coaching, performance plans, or other appropriate steps.
- Inform the President if the matter may escalate to formal discipline or termination of a senior or visible staff member.

### 8.3 Safety & Child Protection

**Examples:**

- Any allegation or risk of:
- Physical, emotional, or sexual abuse of a child.
- Neglect or unsafe environment during the Organization events.
- Medical emergencies or unsafe facilities.

**Immediate staff actions:**

1. Ensure **immediate safety** of individuals involved.
2. Call emergency services (911) if needed.
3. Follow any **mandated reporter** obligations under state law:
- If you are a mandated reporter, you must report directly to the appropriate authorities.
4. Notify the **ED immediately** (or the President of the Board if the ED is unavailable or implicated).

**ED responsibilities:**

- Confirm that all legal reporting obligations are met.
- Notify the President of the Board **as soon as practicable, and no later than 24 hours**.
- Coordinate with legal counsel and appropriate authorities.
- Manage internal communications and ensure confidentiality.

### 8.4 Finance, Fraud & Misuse of Assets

**Examples:**

- Suspected theft or fraud.
- Misuse of the Organization funds or assets.
- Unapproved or suspicious financial transactions.
- Inaccurate or misleading financial reporting.

**Staff responsibilities:**

- Report to the **ED** and **Treasurer immediately** upon suspicion or discovery.
- Preserve relevant documents and records.

**ED responsibilities:**

- Notify the **President and Treasurer** promptly (within 24 hours).
- With the Treasurer:
- Initiate an internal review.
- Engage external accountants or auditors if warranted.
- Bring material issues to the **Executive Committee and/or Board** at the earliest opportunity.

### 8.5 Legal & Compliance Issues

**Examples:**

- Receipt of legal notices, subpoenas, or complaints.
- Regulatory inquiries or investigations.
- Potential violations of laws (e.g., employment, tax, data protection).

**Staff responsibilities:**

- Forward any legal documents to the **ED** immediately.
- Do not respond directly to external legal or regulatory contacts unless directed by the ED or legal counsel.

**ED responsibilities:**

- Notify the **President** within 24 hours.
- Coordinate with legal counsel before any response.
- Determine whether to notify the full Board or Executive Committee, based on seriousness and potential impact.

### 8.6 Reputational & Communications Issues

**Examples:**

- Negative media coverage or social media campaigns about the Organization.
- Public criticism by partners, funders, or prominent advocates.
- Online harassment or misinformation affecting the Organization, staff, or Board members.

**Staff responsibilities:**

- Immediately notify the **ED** (and avoid posting or responding on personal or organizational accounts unless instructed).
- Preserve screenshots or links as evidence.

**ED responsibilities:**

- Inform the **President** promptly (within 24 hours or sooner, depending on severity).
- Propose a communication and response plan.
- Seek the President’s (and where possible, Executive Committee’s) input before issuing public statements.

## 9. Timelines & Expected Response Times

For staff and contractors, the following timelines apply unless there is a more urgent situation:

- Immediate (within hours)
- Safety/child protection emergencies.
- Serious HR incidents involving potential harassment, discrimination, or threats.
- Suspected fraud or major financial irregularities.
- Legal notices, subpoenas, or regulatory contacts.
- Reputational crises gaining public or media traction.
- Within 24 Hours
- Non-emergency but serious HR issues.
- Known or suspected confidentiality breaches.
- Issues involving major donors or partners that may affect relationships or funding.
- Within 3 Business Days
- Routine HR or interpersonal issues.
- Non-urgent financial variances or minor contract questions.
- Programmatic issues affecting multiple stakeholders but not urgent safety or legal concerns.

The ED should acknowledge receipt of significant escalations within **1 business day** and provide a preliminary plan or next steps within **3 business days**, unless circumstances require a faster response.

## 10. Using This Guide in Daily Work

- Staff and contractors should:
- Use this guide to understand when they can proceed and when to seek approval.
- Retain copies of key approvals (e.g., email confirmations) in the Organization’s shared systems.
- Err on the side of raising questions to their supervisor or the ED.
- The ED should:
- Reference this guide when communicating decisions and expectations.
- Provide regular updates to staff if Board policies or thresholds change.
- Work with the President and Treasurer to ensure financial and HR practices stay aligned with Board direction.
