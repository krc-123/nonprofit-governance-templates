# the Organization Executive Director Scope & Escalation (Board Guide)

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Board Guide sets out how the the Organization Board of Directors and Executive Committee delegate authority to, oversee, and receive escalations from the Executive Director (ED), in alignment with:

- the Organization’s Bylaws , including:
- The Board’s ultimate authority over the affairs and property of the Organization.
- The Executive Director’s role, reporting relationship, and ex officio status.
- The Executive Committee’s limited authority between Board meetings.
- The the Organization Employment Agreement for the Executive Director, including:
- The at-will nature of employment and notice practices.
- Duties and reporting lines.
- Section 6.14 regarding Board Executive Committee approval for retention of other individuals.

This document is Board-facing. It is intended to:

- Clarify reserved powers of the Board and Executive Committee.
- Define the ED’s delegated authority and decision-rights.
- Establish approval thresholds and RACI for key functions.
- Set expectations for reporting cadence and dashboards.
- Provide emergency escalation protocols.

This Guide does not amend the Bylaws or the ED’s Employment Agreement. Where conflict exists, the Bylaws and Agreement control.

## 2. Governance Context and ED Role

### 2.1 Board Authority

Under the Bylaws, the Board of Directors:

- Governs the affairs and property of the Organization.
- May hire or contract with an Executive Director to manage and conduct the business of the Organization.
- Retains authority to remove the Executive Director by majority vote of Board Members then in office.
- Acts by majority vote of Board Members present when a quorum is established, unless higher thresholds apply.

### 2.2 Executive Director Role and Status

Per the Bylaws and Employment Agreement:

- The ED manages and conducts the business of the Organization.
- The ED reports to the President of the Board (and, through the President, to the Board).
- The ED serves as an ex officio, non-voting member of:
- The Board of Directors.
- All Board committees, including the Executive Committee.
- The ED:
- Provides monthly activity reports.
- Publishes the Organization’s Annual Report.
- Implements Board-approved strategy, programs, and policies.
- Ensures programmatic excellence and quality of finance, administration, fundraising, communications, and systems.
- Engages volunteers, Board Members, partners, and funders.
- Collaborates with the Treasurer and Finance Committee on budgets and financial health.
- Serves on all Board committees as a non-voting member and supports Board governance work.

### 2.3 Executive Committee Authority (Between Board Meetings)

Per the Bylaws:

- The Executive Committee includes: President, Vice President, Past President, Secretary, Treasurer, and Executive Director (non‑voting).
- In the absence of Board action and between Board meetings, the Executive Committee may:
- Execute customary or required business operations.
- Implement established policies and mandates of the Board.
- The Executive Committee may not:
- Amend the Bylaws.
- Overturn prior Board actions.
- All Executive Committee actions must be ratified at the next Board meeting.

This Guide further clarifies where the Executive Committee has specific approval roles regarding the ED (e.g., retention of other individuals under the ED employment agreement).

## 3. Delegation Principles

The Board delegates authority to the ED based on the following principles:

1. Board AccountabilityThe Board remains collectively accountable for the Organization’s mission, legal compliance, financial health, and reputation. Delegation does not diminish this responsibility.
2. Clarity of Decision RightsThe ED has authority to act within Board-approved plans, budgets, and policies, up to defined thresholds. Material deviations require Board or Executive Committee approval.
3. Transparency and No SurprisesThe ED keeps the President and Board informed on all material matters. Emerging risks, crises, or sensitive issues are escalated promptly as outlined herein.
4. Alignment with Bylaws and ED AgreementDelegation is consistent with:
- ED at-will status.
- Reporting to the President/Board.
- Ex officio, non-voting governance role.
- Required Executive Committee approvals (e.g., retention approvals under the ED employment agreement).
5. Risk‑Based OversightThe Board spends more time on:
- Strategy and impact.
- Financial sustainability.
- Executive performance and succession.
- High‑risk decisions and crises.

## 4. Reserved Powers of the Board and Executive Committee

### 4.1 Reserved Powers of the Board (Non‑Delegable)

The following decisions are reserved to the full Board and may not be delegated to staff. The Executive Committee may act only to the extent permitted by Bylaws and subject to later Board ratification.

1. Mission, Vision, and Strategy
- Approving or materially changing the Organization’s mission and core beliefs.
- Approving multi‑year strategic plans, including major programmatic shifts or national expansion into new markets.
2. Bylaws and Governance Structure
- Amending the Bylaws.
- Changing Board size, composition, or officer structure, as allowed by Bylaws.
- Establishing or disbanding standing committees (beyond those required by Bylaws).
3. Executive Director Employment
- Hiring, renewing, or ending the ED’s Employment Agreement.
- Setting or changing ED compensation and benefits.
- Evaluating ED performance and determining corrective actions or support plans.
- Removing the ED (termination with or without cause) by majority vote, as allowed by Bylaws and the Agreement.
- Approving significant changes to the ED role description or scope.
4. Senior Governance Policies
- Approving or materially revising:
- Conflict of Interest policy.
- Code of Ethics.
- Whistleblower and anti-retaliation policies.
- Risk management and crisis response frameworks.
- Policies governing Board–staff boundaries and communications.
5. Financial Stewardship and Organizational Risk
- Approving the annual operating budget and any material revisions (e.g., cumulative changes exceeding 10% of total budget or 20% of any major program line).
- Approving:
- New or renewed loans or lines of credit.
- Major capital expenditures above the thresholds defined in Section 6.
- Entering into, amending, or terminating leases or agreements that commit the Organization beyond 24 months or above the thresholds in Section 6.
- Approving annual audited financial statements and auditor selection/rotation (where audits are conducted).
- Authorizing any legal action or settlement above defined thresholds or implicating the Organization’s mission, tax-exempt status, or governance integrity.
6. Organizational Form and Dissolution
- Approving:
- Merger or affiliation that changes the Organization’s corporate control or 501(c)(3) status.
- Dissolution, consistent with the Bylaws and Certificate of Incorporation.
- Disposal of all or substantially all the Organization assets or transfer of core programs.

### 4.2 Reserved Powers of the Executive Committee (as a Body)

In addition to its standing authority in the Bylaws, the Executive Committee has specific roles vis‑à‑vis the ED:

1. Retention of Other Individuals (per the ED employment agreement)
- Approving, in writing, any retention of “other individuals” the ED desires to hire to assist in performing ED duties (e.g., consultants, contractors, secondees) where:
- The individual will functionally augment the ED’s own role (e.g., interim deputy ED, senior advisor), or
- The arrangement could be perceived as governance-sensitive (e.g., family members; former ED; high-profile public-facing consultants).
2. Interim Decisions Between Board Meetings
- Approving urgent or time-sensitive actions that:
- Are consistent with Board-approved budgets and policies, but
- Exceed ED’s authority thresholds; and
- Cannot wait until the next regular Board meeting.
- All such actions must be documented and presented for ratification at the next Board meeting.
3. Interim Performance Oversight and Support
- Reviewing ED performance concerns raised by the President, committee chairs, or other Board Members between meetings.
- Authorizing interim corrective or support measures (e.g., coaching, modified goals) pending full Board discussion, where time-sensitive.

## 5. Executive Director Delegated Authority

The ED is empowered to lead and manage the Organization’s day‑to‑day operations within Board‑approved strategies, policies, and budgets.

### 5.1 Strategic and Programmatic Management

The ED is authorized to:

- Implement Board‑approved strategic and annual operating plans.
- Design, plan, implement, and evaluate programs, including:
- Annual conference.
- ASL immersion trainings.
- Other mission‑aligned activities.
- Enter into programmatic partnerships, MOUs, or grants consistent with:
- Approved mission and priorities.
- Budget authority and thresholds (Section 6).
- Make operational adjustments (e.g., scheduling, venues, formats) to achieve Board‑approved goals, provided:
- Material shifts in program model, target population, or national footprint are discussed with the President and, as appropriate, brought to the Board.

### 5.2 Staff and Contractor Management

Within budget and policy constraints, the ED is authorized to:

- Hire, supervise, evaluate, discipline, promote, or terminate staff and contractors, except:
- The ED may not unilaterally retain “other individuals” to assist in performing her duties when the ED employment agreement requires Executive Committee approval.
- Define organizational structure and reporting lines below the ED level.
- Set individual staff goals, performance standards, and development plans.
- Implement non‑discriminatory HR practices aligned with the Organization policies and law.
- Engage routine consultants and vendors (e.g., IT support, event planners, designers) within spending thresholds.

### 5.3 Financial and Administrative Management

Subject to the Board‑approved annual budget and financial policies, the ED is authorized to:

- Manage day‑to‑day finances, including:
- Approving routine operational expenses.
- Overseeing accounts payable and receivable.
- Coordinating with the Treasurer and Finance Committee to maintain cash flow and reserves.
- Oversee financial systems, accounting procedures, and internal controls (with the Treasurer and Finance Committee).
- Coordinate the preparation of:
- Annual budget (for Board approval).
- Interim financial statements and management dashboards.
- Execute Board‑approved financial transactions (e.g., grant agreements, loans or leases explicitly approved by the Board).

### 5.4 Fundraising, Communications, and External Relations

The ED is authorized to:

- Plan and lead fundraising strategies and campaigns within the Organization’s mission and ethical guidelines.
- Manage donor relations and grantor communication, in partnership with the President and designated Board Members for key institutional donors.
- Oversee marketing, public relations, and public communications, including:
- Website, social media, newsletters, and press releases.
- Representing the Organization in external meetings, conferences, and media, consistent with Board‑approved positions.
- Collaborate with Board officers and committees on:
- Fundraising initiatives.
- Advocacy and awareness campaigns.
- Strategic partnerships and alliances.

## 6. Financial and Contractual Approval Thresholds

The following authority levels apply unless superseded by Board resolutions or a formal financial policies manual. All limits are per transaction or commitment, inclusive of all related terms (e.g., renewals, options) and must remain within the Board-approved budget.

### 6.1 Operating Expenditures (Non‑Payroll)

1. Within Approved Budget, Single-Year Commitments
- ED may approve:
- Up to \$10,000 per transaction for budgeted expenses (e.g., program expenses, travel, technology, routine professional services).
- ED plus Treasurer approval required:
- \$10,000 and ≤ \$25,000.
- Board approval required:
- \$25,000 or where commitment meaningfully alters the Board-approved budget (e.g., cumulative variance >10% of a major budget line).
2. Unbudgeted or Materially Over‑Budget Items
- ED may approve:
- Up to \$5,000 per unbudgeted item, if:
- It does not exceed 1% of total annual budget, and
- ED promptly reports the variance in the next monthly report.
- Executive Committee approval required:
- \$5,000 and ≤ \$15,000 for unbudgeted items, subject to later Board ratification.
- Board approval required:
- \$15,000 or where cumulative unbudgeted items exceed 3% of the total annual budget.

### 6.2 Contracts and Agreements

1. Programmatic or Service Contracts (e.g., vendors, consultants)
- ED may sign:
- Contracts ≤ 12 months and ≤ \$10,000 total value, if within budget.
- ED and Treasurer co‑authorization recommended (and required for financial systems and accounting firms) for:
- Contracts > \$10,000 and ≤ \$25,000, within budget, ≤ 24 months.
- Board approval required:
- Contracts > \$25,000, or
- Terms > 24 months, or
- Long‑term commitments affecting the Organization’s strategic direction, risk profile, or location (e.g., office lease).
2. Leases, Loans, and Long‑Term Obligations
- ED may:
- Negotiate terms and conduct due diligence.
- Board approval required before execution of:
- Any lease or financing arrangement exceeding the limits above, or
- Any instrument secured by key organizational assets.

### 6.3 Hiring, Compensation, and Retention of Other Individuals

1. Staff and Routine Contractors
- ED may:
- Hire positions and contractors within the approved staffing budget and salary bands.
- Set compensation consistent with approved ranges and HR policies.
- ED must:
- Notify the Executive Committee of any significant reorganization or new senior role reporting directly to the ED.
2. Retention of Other Individuals (per the ED employment agreement)
- Any retention of “other individuals” to assist the ED in performing her duties (e.g., personal executive consultant, special advisor functioning at ED level, or other unique governance-sensitive roles) requires:
- Written approval by the Executive Committee.
- The ED should provide:
- A short role description.
- Rationale for the retention.
- Proposed cost and funding source.
- Proposed term and supervision structure.
3. Changes to ED’s Own Compensation or Terms
- ED has no authority to approve changes to her own compensation, contract term, or material conditions.
- Such decisions are reserved to the Board (see Section 4.1).

## 8. Reporting Cadence and Dashboards

### 8.1 Monthly ED Report (to President and Board)

In line with Bylaws requiring monthly activity reports, the ED will provide a concise written report each month that includes:

1. Program & Mission
- Key program activities and outcomes.
- Progress against annual goals and strategic plan milestones.
- Emerging opportunities or concerns.
2. Finance
- Income and expenses vs. budget (for month and year‑to‑date).
- Cash position and forecast (at least 90 days out).
- Notable variances and corrective actions.
3. Fundraising & Development
- Donations and grants secured, in pipeline, or at risk.
- Institutional donor updates (especially key partners such as fraternities, corporate sponsors, etc.).
- Upcoming grant deadlines and campaigns.
4. HR & Operations
- Staffing changes (hiring, departures, role changes).
- Key HR issues (without breaching individual confidentiality).
- Operational updates (IT, systems, facilities, vendors).
5. Risk & Compliance
- New or evolving risks (finance, legal, safety, reputation).
- Incidents and how they were managed.
- Any potential conflicts of interest raised or resolved.

### 8.2 Quarterly Dashboards

At least quarterly, the ED will provide a more structured dashboard, aligned to Board priorities, including:

1. Strategic Impact Dashboard
- Program reach and outcomes (e.g., families served, events delivered, geographic reach).
- Progress on strategic initiatives (e.g., national expansion, new program launches).
2. Financial Dashboard
- Budget vs. actual by major category.
- Cash reserves vs. Board policy.
- Fundraising performance vs. targets.
3. Organizational Health Dashboard
- Staff headcount and turnover.
- Engagement indicators (if any internal surveys or feedback exist).
- Diversity, equity, and inclusion indicators (as available and appropriate).
4. Risk and Compliance Dashboard
- Top risks with changes in risk level and mitigation status.
- Any legal, regulatory, or insurance issues.

### 8.3 Annual Reporting

The ED will:

- Coordinate preparation and publication of the Annual Report (as required by Bylaws).
- Support preparation of:
- Annual budget for Board approval.
- Any external financial, compliance, or grant reports.
- A comprehensive year‑in‑review presentation to the Board, synthesizing:
- Mission impact.
- Financial performance.
- Fundraising and partnerships.
- Organizational learning and priorities for the coming year.

## 9. Emergency Escalation Protocols

The ED has a duty to promptly escalate issues that pose a material threat to the Organization’s mission, legal status, finances, reputation, or safety.

### 9.1 Trigger Categories

The ED must treat the following as potential emergencies requiring rapid escalation:

1. Safety and Welfare
- Any incident involving immediate or credible threats to physical safety of staff, volunteers, families, or participants at the Organization events.
2. Financial Distress or Irregularities
- Actual or projected inability to meet obligations (e.g., payroll, key vendor payments) within 60 days.
- Suspected fraud, embezzlement, or material financial misstatements.
- Unapproved or uncovered commitments above Board-defined thresholds.
3. Legal and Regulatory Issues
- Actual or threatened litigation.
- Government or regulatory inquiries or investigations.
- Issues potentially affecting the Organization’s 501(c)(3) status or eligibility for key grants.
4. Reputational Crises
- Media coverage or social media incidents that:
- Misrepresent or question the Organization’s integrity, inclusivity, or treatment of the communities it serves.
- Involve allegations of discrimination, abuse, harassment, or misconduct.
- Public campaigns or complaints involving major donors or partners.
5. Executive Leadership Concerns
- ED is incapacitated, unavailable, or subject to serious allegations (e.g., harassment, fraud, gross misconduct).
- Senior staff or key volunteer resignations that materially impair operations.

### 9.2 Escalation Path and Timelines

1. Immediate Notification (Within 24 Hours)
- ED must notify the President as soon as practicable (ideally within 24 hours) when any of the triggers arise.
- If the President is unavailable within 24 hours, ED must notify:
- The Vice President; and
- At least one additional officer (e.g., Treasurer or Secretary).
2. Secondary Notification (Within 48 Hours)
- President (or designee) will:
- Assess the situation with the ED.
- Decide whether to:
- Convene the Executive Committee.
- Notify the full Board promptly via email or other agreed means.
- In high‑severity cases (e.g., threats to safety, solvency, or tax status), the President should:
- Convene an emergency Executive Committee meeting as soon as possible.
- Consult Board counsel or other appropriate professionals.
3. Emergency Board Action
- The President may:
- Call an emergency Board meeting via teleconference or video per Bylaws provisions.
- Or, where appropriate, proceed via unanimous written consent for urgent decisions.
- The ED will:
- Prepare a concise briefing (1–2 pages) with:
- What happened.
- Known facts and uncertainties.
- Immediate actions taken.
- Proposed options and recommendations.
- External communication needs (if any).

### 9.3 Roles During Emergencies

1. Executive Director
- Maintains operational control of staff and programs to the extent safe and lawful.
- Implements immediate risk‑mitigation steps (e.g., suspending an event, isolating systems, placing staff on leave).
- Prepares documentation and updates for the President, Executive Committee, and Board.
- Coordinates with external experts (e.g., legal counsel, PR advisors) as authorized.
2. President and Executive Committee
- Provide real‑time guidance to the ED.
- Make interim decisions within the Bylaws‑defined authority, subject to Board ratification.
- Decide if independent investigation or external review is required.
3. Board of Directors
- Receives clear, timely updates.
- Makes final decisions on:
- Direction of investigations.
- Public statements on sensitive matters.
- Material settlements.
- Leadership changes or disciplinary actions involving the ED or senior personnel.
- Documents all major decisions and rationales in Board minutes.

### 9.4 Communications in Emergencies

- The ED will:
- Coordinate with the President regarding any external communications.
- Ensure internal staff communications are timely, accurate, and respectful, while maintaining confidentiality where necessary.
- The President (or a designee such as the Communications Committee Chair) will:
- Serve as the primary spokesperson to media on governance‑sensitive issues.
- Approve final versions of high‑risk public statements.
- The ED will:
- Refrain from unilateral external statements on crises involving governance, Board conduct, or the ED’s own employment, unless:
- Required by law, subpoena, or regulatory authority; or
- Immediate safety requires communication before Board consultation (in which case the ED must notify the President at the earliest opportunity).

## 10. Implementation, Review, and Amendments

### 10.1 Adoption and Orientation

- Upon Board approval, this Guide:
- Becomes a governing policy document informing Board–ED interactions.
- Is provided to:
- All current and incoming Board Members.
- The Executive Director.
- Orientation:
- New Board Members receive an overview of this Guide as part of Board onboarding.
- The ED reviews this Guide with any senior staff who regularly interact with the Board.

### 10.2 Annual Review and Continuous Improvement

- The President, in collaboration with the ED and Executive Committee, will:
- Review this Guide at least annually, or sooner if:
- Bylaws are amended.
- The ED Employment Agreement is materially revised.
- Significant governance lessons arise (e.g., following a crisis or major transition).
- Proposed revisions:
- Are drafted by the President (or a Governance/Bylaws Committee).
- Are reviewed with the ED to ensure operational clarity.
- Are approved by the Board via majority vote (as a policy, not as a Bylaw amendment).

### 10.3 Alignment with Other Documents

- This Guide is intended to be consistent with:
- the Organization Bylaws (current and future revisions).
- ED Employment Agreement and any amendments.
- Other Board‑approved policies (e.g., Conflict of Interest, Code of Ethics, Whistleblower, HR and Financial policies).
- In case of conflict:
- The Bylaws control over this Guide.
- The ED Employment Agreement controls over this Guide with respect to ED employment terms.
- The Board may update this Guide after any Bylaw or Agreement change to restore clarity and alignment.

## 11. Summary of Key Expectations for the Board and ED

**For the Board:**

- Focus on:
- Mission, strategy, and results.
- Financial stewardship and risk oversight.
- ED selection, support, and evaluation.
- Respect:
- Delegated management authority of the ED within approved plans and thresholds.
- Uphold:
- Timely, clear communication with the ED.
- Consistent use of this Guide in decision‑making and escalations.

**For the Executive Director:**

- Operate within:
- Board‑approved strategy, budget, and policy.
- Authority limits and thresholds defined in this Guide.
- Maintain:
- Transparent, proactive communication with the President and Board.
- Regular, structured reporting and dashboards.
- Escalate:
- Emergencies, high‑risk issues, and governance‑sensitive matters in accordance with Section 9.
- Seek:
- Executive Committee approval for retention of “other individuals” where required by the ED employment agreement.
- Board guidance where decisions exceed authority or have major strategic implications.

By following this Scope & Escalation framework, the Organization’s Board and Executive Director can maintain clear roles, timely oversight, and effective collaboration in service of the Organization’s mission.
