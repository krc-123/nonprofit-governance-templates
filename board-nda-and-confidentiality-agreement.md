# the Organization Board NDA & Confidentiality Agreement (2026 Draft)

**Adopted by the Board of Directors effective:** [Effective date] **Organization:**[Organization Name] (the “Organization”) **Jurisdiction of Incorporation:**[State of Incorporation]

## 1. Purpose and Scope

The purpose of this the Organization Board NDA & Confidentiality Agreement (this “Agreement”) is to:

1. Protect the Organization’s confidential, sensitive, and proprietary information, including information related to the children, youth, and families in the Organization’s programs, the Organization’s donors and members, personnel, and operations.
2. Clarify the obligations of each member of the Board of Directors (each, a “Director”) regarding the use, disclosure, safeguarding, and return of the Organization information and materials.
3. Confirm that these confidentiality obligations are intended to be consistent with, and not in derogation of, Directors’ fiduciary duties of care, loyalty, and obedience under Delaware law and the Organization’s governing documents.
4. Provide clear carveouts for legally required disclosures, whistleblower and regulatory reporting, and public-records obligations where applicable.

This Agreement applies to all voting and non-voting members of the Board of Directors, and to any Board observer or advisor that the Board designates as subject to this Agreement.

By signing this Agreement, the Director agrees to be bound by its terms as a condition of Board service or other authorized participation in the Organization Board activities.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 “Confidential Information”

For purposes of this Agreement, “Confidential Information” means any non-public information, in whatever form or medium (oral, written, electronic, visual, or otherwise), that is disclosed or made available to the Director in connection with the Organization Board or committee service, including but not limited to:

1. Board and Governance Materials
- Board and committee agendas, minutes, packets, pre-reads, discussion drafts, resolutions, strategy documents, and internal reports.
- Executive session discussions and materials.
- Board evaluation materials and individual Director feedback.
2. Donor, Fundraising, and Member Information
- Donor, sponsor, and funder identities, giving histories, capacities, cultivation and stewardship plans, and grant proposals or reports.
- Membership lists and databases, member contact and demographic information, and records of member interactions with the Organization.
- Terms of major gifts, grants, sponsorships, or restricted funds.
3. Program, Participant, and Family Information
- Non-public information about the Organization’s programs, curricula, partnerships, and evaluation methods.
- Any information that could reasonably identify children, youth, or families connected with the Organization’s programs (including names, contact information, diagnoses, communication preferences, participation records, or feedback), whether or not such information is subject to specific privacy laws.
- Risk, safety, or safeguarding reports or investigations related to children, families, or participants.
4. Personnel, HR, and Internal Operations
- Information about employees, contractors, and volunteers (including applications, evaluations, compensation, HR files, performance or disciplinary issues, and internal investigations).
- Organizational charts, staffing plans, succession plans, and internal restructuring discussions.
- Internal policies, standard operating procedures, and drafts under development.
5. Financial, Legal, and Strategic Information
- Non-public financial data, budgets, forecasts, audit and management letters, internal controls, and banking or investment information.
- Legal advice and attorney-client communications (whether to the Organization, the Board, or a Board committee), including litigation, claims, threats, investigations, or regulatory matters.
- Strategic plans, business models, and non-public partnership or contract negotiations.
6. Technical, IT, and Data Security Information
- System access credentials, security protocols, and vendor or platform configurations.
- Any information that could reasonably be used to compromise the Organization’s systems or data privacy.
7. Third-Party Information
- Confidential information of the Organization partners, vendors, donors, or other third parties that the Organization is obligated to protect, when shared with the Director in their Board role.

### 2.2 Exclusions

Confidential Information does**not**include information that the Director can demonstrate, through written records or other reliable evidence:

1. Was already lawfully known to the Director in a non-confidential capacity before it was received from the Organization in the Director’s Board role.
2. Became publicly available without breach of this Agreement or any other legal or contractual duty owed by the Director or the Organization.
3. Was lawfully obtained by the Director, on a non-confidential basis, from a third party who did not acquire or disclose it in violation of any duty of confidentiality to the Organization or another party.
4. Is expressly designated in writing by the Organization as non-confidential or publicly shareable for a particular purpose.

Information is not excluded from Confidential Information merely because individual elements of it are publicly known if the compilation, aggregation, or context provided by the Organization remains non-public and sensitive.

## 3. Duty of Confidentiality

### 3.1 Core Obligation

The Director shall:

1. Use Confidential Information solely for the purpose of fulfilling the Director’s Board or committee responsibilities for the Organization.
2. Not disclose Confidential Information to any person or entity, except as expressly permitted under Section 4 (Permitted Disclosures).
3. Exercise at least the same degree of care in safeguarding Confidential Information that the Director uses to protect their own sensitive information, and in any event no less than a reasonable standard of care for a Director of a [State] nonprofit corporation.

### 3.2 Board Materials and Deliberations

Without limiting the generality of Section 3.1, the Director specifically agrees:

1. Not to disclose the content of Board or committee discussions, including votes, deliberations, and individual Director positions, except where the Board or its Chair (or another officer expressly authorized by the Board) has approved a specific communication.
2. To treat executive session materials and discussions as strictly confidential and limit any references to them solely to other Directors and persons who are expressly authorized to participate in that executive session.
3. To refrain from recording Board or committee meetings (audio, video, or screenshots) unless expressly authorized by the Board and subject to the Organization’s consent, privacy, and safeguarding practices.

### 3.3 Donor, Member, and Personnel Information

The Director acknowledges that the Organization’s donors, members, employees, contractors, volunteers, and families rely on the Organization to protect their privacy and trust. Accordingly, the Director:

1. Will not use donor, member, or personnel information for any personal, political, commercial, or other non-the Organization purpose.
2. Will not share donor, member, participant, or personnel information (including contact information or giving histories) with any third party for fundraising, marketing, or advocacy unrelated to the Organization, absent explicit authorization from the Board or the Executive Director consistent with Board-approved policy.
3. Will be especially careful with any information related to children, families, or safeguarding concerns, and will handle such information strictly on a need-to-know basis and in accordance with applicable laws and Board-approved policies.

## 4. Permitted Disclosures

Subject to the duty of confidentiality and any applicable conflicts or recusal obligations, the Director may disclose Confidential Information only as follows:

### 4.1 Disclosures Within the Organization

The Director may share Confidential Information with:

1. Other the Organization Directors and Board officers, for legitimate Board or committee purposes.
2. The Executive Director and the Organization staff who have a legitimate need to know the information to perform their official duties, consistent with the Organization policies and directives.
3. Board committees and task forces, and their authorized advisors or consultants, where such disclosure is reasonably necessary for the committee’s assigned work and consistent with any applicable committee charter or Board authorization.

### 4.2 Advisors Subject to Confidentiality Obligations

The Director may, as reasonably necessary to fulfill Board obligations, disclose Confidential Information to:

1. Legal counsel engaged by the Organization, the Board, or a Board committee.
2. Other professional advisors expressly engaged or authorized by the Organization (e.g., accountants, auditors, consultants), where disclosure is needed for them to provide advice or services to the Organization.

The Director will reasonably confirm that such advisors are subject to appropriate professional or contractual confidentiality obligations before sharing Confidential Information.

### 4.3 Legally Required Disclosures

Where the Director is**required by law, subpoena, court order, regulatory demand, or other compulsory legal process**to disclose Confidential Information, the Director may make such disclosure, provided that:

1. To the extent permitted by law, the Director promptly notifies the Board Chair (or if the Chair is implicated, the Vice Chair or another officer designated by the Board) and the Organization’s Executive Director of the request or requirement, so that the Organization may seek protective measures or otherwise respond.
2. The Director discloses only the portion of Confidential Information that is legally required, and uses reasonable efforts to preserve confidentiality (for example, by requesting confidential or sealed treatment where appropriate).

### 4.4 Whistleblower and Regulatory Reporting Carveout

Nothing in this Agreement:

1. Prohibits or restricts the Director from reporting in good faith a suspected violation of law, regulation, or fiduciary duty; financial impropriety; fraud; harassment or discrimination; abuse or neglect of a child or vulnerable person; or other serious misconduct to:
- A government agency, law enforcement body, or court;
- A state or federal regulator;
- A mandatory reporting hotline or equivalent authority; or
- Legal counsel retained by the Director individually for purposes of obtaining advice on their rights or obligations.
2. Requires the Director to notify the Organization before making such a report, where notice is prohibited by law or might reasonably be expected to undermine the effectiveness of the report or expose the Director to retaliation.

The Director agrees, however, that to the extent reasonably practicable and lawful, they will limit any disclosure of the Organization Confidential Information in such contexts to what is necessary to make or support the report or to respond to inquiries from the relevant authority.

### 4.5 Communication of Board Decisions

Consistent with the Organization’s Bylaws, Board policies, and the Board Code of Conduct, nothing in this Agreement prevents:

1. The Board, through the Chair, Executive Director, or other designated spokesperson, from communicating board-approved decisions, policies, or messages to members, donors, media, or the public.
2. The Director from referring to publicly announced Board decisions or the Organization positions, provided that the Director does not:
- Disclose or characterize confidential deliberations leading to that decision; or
- Represent that they speak on behalf of the Organization unless expressly authorized to do so.

## 5. Secure Handling, Storage, and Return of Materials

### 5.1 Secure Handling and Storage

The Director shall handle and store Confidential Information in a manner designed to prevent unauthorized access, use, or disclosure, including by:

1. Using password-protected and reasonably secure devices and accounts for all electronic Board and committee materials.
2. Avoiding forwarding the Organization materials to personal or work accounts that do not meet comparable security standards, unless reasonably necessary and appropriately safeguarded.
3. Storing physical documents in a secure location (e.g., a locked drawer or cabinet) and not leaving them unattended in public or shared spaces.
4. Refraining from using unencrypted flash drives or insecure file-sharing services for Confidential Information, except where approved or provided by the Organization.

### 5.2 Reporting Suspected Breaches

If the Director becomes aware of or reasonably suspects:

1. Any loss, theft, or unauthorized access to devices or accounts containing the Organization Confidential Information; or
2. Any unauthorized disclosure or misuse of Confidential Information,

the Director shall promptly notify the Board Chair (or, if the Chair is implicated, another officer designated by the Board) and the Executive Director and cooperate in the Organization’s efforts to investigate, mitigate, and remediate the issue.

### 5.3 Return or Destruction of Materials

Upon:

1. The end of the Director’s Board service (including resignation, removal, or expiration of term without renewal); or
2. The Organization’s written request, where reasonably related to a specific issue (for example, litigation, investigation, or other regulatory matter),

the Director shall, within a reasonable time:

1. Return to the Organization or securely destroy (at the Organization’s option) all physical documents containing Confidential Information that are within the Director’s possession, custody, or control, other than those required to be retained by law or by a valid legal process.
2. Delete or permanently disable access to electronic Board and committee materials (including from personal devices and accounts), except where:
- Stored in the Organization-managed systems to which the Organization can centrally control access; or
- Retention is required by law or a legal hold.

If destruction is requested, the Director shall, upon reasonable request, certify in writing that such destruction has been completed, subject to any legal retention requirements.

## 6. Duration of Obligations

### 6.1 Term of Agreement

This Agreement becomes effective on the date the Director signs it and remains in effect:

1. For the duration of the Director’s service on the the Organization Board (including any reappointments or extended terms); and
2. Thereafter,**with respect to all Confidential Information**, for so long as the information remains non-public, or for any longer period required by applicable law or contract.

### 6.2 Continuing Obligations After Board Service

The Director’s obligations under Sections 3 (Duty of Confidentiality), 4 (Permitted Disclosures), and 5 (Secure Handling, Storage, and Return of Materials) survive:

1. The Director’s resignation or removal from the Board;
2. Any expiration or non-renewal of the Director’s term; and
3. Any termination of this Agreement by the Organization, except where the Organization expressly releases the Director from specific confidentiality obligations in writing.

## 7. Public Records, Open Meetings, and Dual-Service Situations

### 7.1 Directors Also Serving in Public Roles

The Organization recognizes that some Directors may also serve as public officials or in roles subject to:

- State or local open records or “freedom of information” laws,
- Open meetings or sunshine laws, or
- Other statutory public-disclosure obligations (collectively, “Public Records Laws”).

Where a Director’s Public Records Law obligations conflict with their confidentiality obligations under this Agreement, the following principles apply:

1. This Agreement is**not intended**to require a Director to violate applicable law.
2. The Director shall nevertheless:
- Inform the Organization (through the Board Chair and Executive Director) of any Public Records Law request or obligation that may require disclosure of the Organization Confidential Information, to the extent permitted by law and practical under the circumstances.
- Use good faith efforts, in coordination with the Organization and its counsel where possible, to assert available exemptions or protections (such as protections for donor identities, personal privacy, child-related information, or trade secrets) that could prevent or limit disclosure of sensitive the Organization information.
- Disclose only the minimum Confidential Information required to comply with the applicable Public Records Law, and seek confidential or redacted treatment where permissible.

### 7.2 the Organization’s Expectation of Good Faith Coordination

The Director agrees to make reasonable, good faith efforts to:

1. Coordinate with the Organization when navigating dual obligations to the Organization and to a public entity or constituency.
2. Communicate early when potential conflicts arise, so that the Organization can participate in evaluating options for protecting Confidential Information within the bounds of applicable law.

## 8. Relationship to Fiduciary Duties and Other the Organization Policies

### 8.1 Fiduciary Duties Unaffected

Nothing in this Agreement is intended to, or shall be construed to:

1. Limit or waive the Director’s fiduciary duties of care, loyalty, and obedience to the Organization under Delaware law or the Organization’s Certificate of Incorporation and Bylaws.
2. Prevent the Director from:
- Exercising their duty of candor and oversight in Board or committee deliberations; or
- Accessing the Organization information to which a Director is lawfully entitled under Delaware law for purposes of fulfilling their duties.

In any apparent conflict between this Agreement and a Director’s mandatory fiduciary duties under Delaware law, the Director shall comply with applicable law, while maintaining the highest practicable level of confidentiality consistent with such compliance.

### 8.2 Interaction with Bylaws and Board Policies

This Agreement is intended to complement and be read consistently with:

- the Organization’s Certificate of Incorporation and Bylaws (as amended from time to time);
- the Organization’s Conflict of Interest Policy and annual disclosure requirements;
- the Organization’s Board Code of Conduct;
- Any Board-approved whistleblower, safeguarding, or data privacy policies; and
- The Executive Director’s employment agreement and related governance documents, to the extent they address Board access to information.

If there is a direct, irreconcilable conflict between this Agreement and:

1. A mandatory provision of Delaware law; or
2. The Organization’s Certificate of Incorporation or Bylaws,

then the applicable law or governing document shall control, and this Agreement shall be interpreted and applied to give effect to the maximum extent of its confidentiality protections that remain consistent with that higher authority.

## 9. Remedies and Enforcement

### 9.1 Internal Board Remedies

If the Board has reasonable cause to believe that a Director has violated this Agreement or otherwise misused Confidential Information, the Board may:

1. Inform the Director of the basis for such belief and provide the Director an opportunity to explain or respond.
2. Investigate the matter, using such internal or external resources as the Board deems appropriate.
3. Take corrective or disciplinary action consistent with the Organization’s Bylaws and Board policies, which may include, without limitation:
- Removal from one or more committees or leadership roles;
- A written admonition or requirement for additional training;
- Recommendation to the membership or appointing body (as applicable) that the Director be removed from the Board; and/or
- Other measures reasonably designed to protect the Organization and deter future violations.

### 9.2 Legal and Equitable Relief

The Organization is likely to suffer irreparable harm from unauthorized disclosures of Confidential Information that cannot be adequately remedied by monetary damages alone. Accordingly:

1. The Organization may seek equitable relief (including temporary, preliminary, or permanent injunctive relief or specific performance) to prevent or address any threatened or actual breach of this Agreement, without the need to post bond beyond any amount required by law.
2. This Section 9.2 does not limit any other remedies that may be available to the Organization at law or in equity.

## 10. Governing Law and Dispute Resolution

### 10.1 Governing Law

This Agreement, and any dispute, claim, or controversy arising out of or relating to this Agreement or the Director’s confidentiality obligations to the Organization, shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the [State of Incorporation], without regard to its conflict-of-laws principles.

### 10.2 Venue

Subject to any mandatory jurisdictional requirements, any legal action or proceeding arising out of or relating to this Agreement shall be brought in a state or federal court located within the [State of Incorporation]. The Director and the Organization consent to the personal jurisdiction of such courts and waive any objection based on forum non conveniens to the extent permitted by law.

## 11. Miscellaneous

### 11.1 Entire Agreement

This Agreement:

1. Sets forth the entire agreement between the Organization and the Director regarding confidentiality of Board-related information; and
2. Supersedes all prior or contemporaneous written or oral understandings on this subject, except that it does not supersede or limit any stricter confidentiality obligations a Director may have under applicable law, other the Organization policies, or separate written agreements (for example, if a Director separately provides professional services to the Organization as a contractor, subject to a separate NDA).

### 11.2 Amendments and Waivers

1. This Agreement may be amended only by a written instrument approved by the Board of Directors and signed by an authorized the Organization officer.
2. No waiver of any provision of this Agreement shall be effective unless in writing and signed by the Organization. A waiver of any breach shall not be deemed a waiver of any other or subsequent breach.

### 11.3 Severability

If any provision of this Agreement is found by a court of competent jurisdiction to be invalid, illegal, or unenforceable, such provision shall be enforced to the maximum extent permissible, and the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

### 11.4 Counterparts and Electronic Signatures

This Agreement may be executed:

1. In counterparts, each of which shall be deemed an original, and all of which together shall constitute one and the same instrument; and
2. By electronic signature, scanned signature, or any other form of signature or assent recognized by applicable law, each of which shall be deemed an original for all purposes.
