# the Organization Staff Conflict of Interest Policy (2026 Draft)

## 1. Purpose and Guiding Principles

The [Organization Name] (the “Organization”) relies on the integrity and good judgment of its staff to advance the Organization’s mission in the best interests of the people it serves.

The purpose of this Staff Conflict of Interest Policy (“Policy”) is to:

- Prevent actual, potential, and perceived conflicts of interest involving the Organization staff.
- Align staff expectations with the Organization’s Board Conflict of Interest Policy and related governance standards.
- Provide clear guidance on gifts, outside employment, vendor relationships, and other situations that may create conflicts.
- Establish consistent procedures for disclosure, review, recusal, documentation, and enforcement.

This Policy supplements, and does not replace, applicable federal, state, or local laws and regulations that govern conflicts of interest and ethical conduct.

All staff are expected to:

- Act in the best interests of the Organization, not for personal gain.
- Avoid situations where personal, financial, or other interests may conflict with the Organization’s interests.
- Disclose actual or potential conflicts promptly and fully.
- Follow all directions given to manage, reduce, or eliminate conflicts.

## 2. Scope and Covered Persons

This Policy applies to all “Covered Staff,” defined as:

- All regular, temporary, full-time, and part-time the Organization employees, including the Executive Director.
- All individuals engaged by the Organization under a contract for services where the individual performs staff-like functions (e.g., ongoing contractors, consultants, or ASL instructors working under an the Organization contract).
- Any other person designated in writing by the Executive Director or the Board (for example, a long-term intern or fellow in a staff-like role).

Volunteers who are not in staff-like roles are primarily covered under separate volunteer policies, but staff must still be alert to conflicts involving volunteers and report concerns under this Policy.

Where this Policy refers to “staff” or “Covered Staff,” it includes all persons described above.

## 3. Key Definitions

For purposes of this Policy:

### 3.1 Conflict of Interest

A “conflict of interest” exists when a Covered Staff member’s personal, financial, or other interests (or those of a Related Party) could reasonably be expected to:

- Influence, or appear to influence, the staff member’s judgment or actions in performing the Organization duties; or
- Interfere with the staff member’s ability to act solely in the best interests of the Organization and its mission.

A conflict of interest may be:

- **Actual**(a specific situation is currently in place),
- **Potential**(a situation could develop based on future events), or
- **Perceived**(a reasonable person could view the situation as a conflict, even if the staff member believes they can remain impartial).

A conflict of interest is not, by itself, evidence of wrongdoing. However, failure to disclose, manage, or comply with guidance regarding a conflict is a violation of this Policy.

### 3.2 Financial Interest

A Covered Staff member has a “Financial Interest” if they, directly or indirectly, through business, investment, or family/relationship, have:

1. **Ownership or investment interest**in any entity with which the Organization has, or is considering, a transaction or arrangement (for example, stock ownership, partnership interest, or other equity).
2. **Compensation arrangement**with the Organization or with any entity or individual that has, or is considering, a transaction or arrangement with the Organization. “Compensation” includes salary, fees, honoraria, commissions, consulting income, royalties, gifts, or other benefits of more than nominal value.
3. **Prospective financial relationship**, such as a potential ownership, investment interest, or arrangement for compensation being discussed or negotiated.
4. **Formal role or duty to another organization**(for example, as an officer, director, employee, or key volunteer) that creates a legal or fiduciary obligation to act in that organization’s best interests when that organization’s interests may conflict with the Organization’s.

A Financial Interest does not automatically mean there is a conflict. It becomes a conflict if it could reasonably influence how the staff member carries out their the Organization responsibilities.

### 3.3 Related Party / Related Person

A “Related Party” (or “Related Person”) includes:

- The staff member’s spouse or domestic partner.
- The staff member’s parents, stepparents, grandparents, children, stepchildren, grandchildren, siblings (including half-siblings), and the spouses or domestic partners of any of these individuals.
- Any entity or trust where the staff member or any of the above relatives:
- Serves as an officer, director, trustee, employee, partner, or key volunteer; or
- Holds a material ownership or beneficial interest (for example, 5% or more for a partnership, or 35% or more for a closely held company or trust).

### 3.4 Gifts and Hospitality

“Gifts and hospitality” include anything of value received from a current or potential vendor, funder, partner, or other third party that interacts with the Organization, including but not limited to:

- Cash, gift cards, or equivalents.
- Tickets, meals, lodging, travel, or entertainment.
- Discounts or special pricing not generally available to the public.
- Tangible items (e.g., electronics, equipment, clothing, artwork).
- Services (e.g., complimentary professional services).

Nominal, routine items (such as low-cost branded items) may be permissible, but must still be evaluated under the standards in Section 7.

## 4. General Duties of Covered Staff

All Covered Staff must:

1. **Act in the Organization’s best interests**and support its mission, values, and policies.
2. **Avoid conflicts**between their personal, financial, or other interests and those of the Organization.
3. **Disclose conflicts**:
- During hiring or onboarding.
- On an annual basis.
- As soon as they become aware of any new or changed situation that may create a conflict.
4. **Recuse themselves**from discussions, decisions, or actions where they have a conflict, unless explicitly authorized in writing under this Policy.
5. **Follow instructions**issued to manage, reduce, or eliminate a conflict, including reassignment of duties or changes to reporting lines.
6. **Maintain confidentiality**of the Organization information and use it only for authorized the Organization business, not for personal benefit or for the benefit of Related Parties.

These duties apply whether or not the staff member believes they can remain objective in a given situation. When in doubt, staff must disclose and seek guidance.

## 5. Common Types of Staff Conflicts of Interest

The following are examples of situations that commonly create conflicts. This list is illustrative, not exhaustive.

### 5.1 Vendor and Procurement Relationships

- Participating in the selection, negotiation, supervision, or payment of a vendor that is owned or controlled by the staff member or a Related Party.
- Steering the Organization business toward a company where the staff member or a Related Party has a Financial Interest.
- Accepting favorable personal terms from a vendor (e.g., discounted personal services) that may be conditioned on the Organization’s continued relationship with that vendor.

### 5.2 Outside Employment, Consulting, and Self‑Employment

- Holding a paid or unpaid position with another organization that:
- Competes with the Organization programs or services.
- Seeks or receives funding from the same donors or funders under circumstances where the staff member’s judgment for the Organization might be influenced.
- Uses materials, contact lists, or confidential information obtained through the Organization without appropriate authorization.
- Maintaining a business (e.g., as a consultant, interpreter, instructor, or content creator) that contracts with the Organization or receives referrals from the Organization programs.

### 5.3 Gifts, Hospitality, and Other Benefits

- Accepting gifts or hospitality from vendors, funders, partners, or families that could reasonably appear to influence the Organization decisions (see Section 7).
- Soliciting gifts, favors, or discounts from vendors or partners because of one’s role at the Organization.

### 5.4 Family and Close Personal Relationships

- Supervising, evaluating, or approving the compensation or contracts of a family member, partner, or close personal friend.
- Participating in hiring or discipline decisions involving a Related Party or romantic partner.
- Assigning work or making scheduling decisions that favor a Related Party over other staff or contractors.

### 5.5 Fundraising, Development, and Sponsorship

- Steering sponsorships or donations toward events or entities where the staff member has a Financial Interest without disclosure.
- Directing donor benefits (e.g., recognition opportunities, complimentary tickets) in ways that benefit the staff member or a Related Party personally, without appropriate approvals.

### 5.6 Use of Confidential Information or Opportunities

- Using confidential the Organization information (e.g., donor lists, grant opportunities, program plans, or proprietary materials) for personal benefit or for the benefit of a Related Party.
- Diverting an opportunity that properly belongs to the Organization (for example, a program partnership or funding opportunity) to another organization or to one’s own business.

### 5.7 Social Media, Endorsements, and Public Representation

- Publicly endorsing a company or product in a way that implies the Organization endorsement when the staff member or a Related Party has a Financial Interest in that company.
- Using the Organization platforms, logos, or events to promote a business or organization with which the staff member or a Related Party is affiliated, without permission and disclosure.

If a staff member is uncertain whether a situation falls within one of these categories or creates a conflict, they must seek guidance and err on the side of disclosure.

## 6. Disclosure Requirements and Timing

### 6.1 At Hiring or Onboarding

As part of hiring or onboarding, each new Covered Staff member must:

- Review this Policy and the current the Organization Board Conflict of Interest Policy.
- Complete an initial Staff Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form identifying any known actual, potential, or perceived conflicts, including:
- Outside employment or consulting.
- Ownership or leadership roles in other organizations that interact or may interact with the Organization.
- Existing vendor or contractor relationships involving the staff member or a Related Party.
- Sign the acknowledgement included in the form confirming that they understand, and agree to comply with, this Policy.

Any identified conflicts must be reviewed and addressed before or promptly upon the start of employment or engagement.

### 6.2 Annual Disclosure (Staff COI Acknowledgement and Short Form)

Each Covered Staff member must:

- Annually review this Policy and any related the Organization Board Conflict of Interest Policy, and
- Complete and sign the the Organization Staff Conflict of Interest Annual Disclosure and Acknowledgement Form.

The annual form is designed to be short and practical and will generally:

- Confirm that the staff member has received, read, and agrees to comply with this Policy.
- Require the staff member to:
- Either check a box stating that they have no reportable conflicts; or
- Disclose any organizations, vendors, or other entities where they or a Related Party have a Financial Interest or significant role that could intersect with the Organization.
- Provide space to describe:
- The nature of the relationship/interest.
- The type of interaction (e.g., vendor, partner, funder, contractor).
- Any steps already being taken to mitigate the conflict.
- Remind the staff member of their duty to update the form as circumstances change.

The annual disclosure process is coordinated by the Executive Director or designee. If a disclosure involves the Executive Director, it will be reviewed under the procedures in Section 8.3.

### 6.3 Ongoing and Event‑Driven Disclosure

Covered Staff must not wait for the annual process to disclose new or changed situations. They must:

- Promptly (as soon as practical, and generally within 10 business days) disclose any new actual, potential, or perceived conflict.
- Update their disclosure if:
- They start or change outside employment or consulting work.
- They or a Related Party gain or lose a Financial Interest in a vendor, partner, or funder interacting with the Organization.
- They begin a personal relationship that may affect supervision, evaluation, or resource allocation at the Organization.
- Any situation previously disclosed materially changes.

Disclosures may be made using the Staff COI Disclosure Form or other written format designated by the Organization, and must be submitted to the appropriate reviewing authority as described in Section 8.

## 7. Gifts, Hospitality, and Other Benefits

The Organization recognizes that modest, appropriate gestures can support relationships; however, gifts and hospitality can also create or appear to create conflicts.

### 7.1 General Rules

1. Covered Staff**may not**request or solicit gifts, hospitality, or other personal benefits from any individual or entity that does or seeks to do business with the Organization.
2. Covered Staff**may not**accept gifts, hospitality, or other benefits that:
- Are intended, or could reasonably be perceived as intended, to influence an the Organization decision or action; or
- Create an obligation or expectation inconsistent with the Organization’s interests or policies.
3. Covered Staff**must immediately decline or return**:
- Cash or cash equivalents (including gift cards) offered by vendors, partners, or funders in connection with the Organization business, except for:
- Modest tokens of appreciation for speaking or presenting where acceptance is consistent with the Organization practices and approved by the Executive Director or designee.
4. Covered Staff must obtain prior written approval from their supervisor (and, where applicable, the Executive Director) before accepting:
- Gifts, hospitality, or benefits with an estimated value**greater than \$100**in connection with the Organization business from any single source in a calendar year.

### 7.2 Nominal and Reasonable Items

Examples that are generally permissible (subject to supervisor notification when in doubt) include:

- Low-value branded items (e.g., mugs, pens, notebooks).
- Modest refreshments (e.g., coffee, snacks) offered during routine meetings.
- Modest, infrequent meals or local hospitality offered in connection with the Organization business, provided there is no expectation of favorable treatment and the value is reasonable.

If a staff member is uncertain whether an item is “nominal” or “reasonable,” they must ask their supervisor or the Executive Director before accepting it.

### 7.3 Gifts From Families, Program Participants, or Volunteers

Because the Organization serves children and families:

- Staff must avoid accepting gifts that could influence, or appear to influence, program decisions, access to services, or special treatment.
- Staff may accept small tokens of appreciation (e.g., handmade items or low-cost gifts) offered by families or volunteers, provided that:
- Acceptance does not violate applicable law or funding restrictions.
- No special treatment is given in return.
- The gift is reported to a supervisor where it is more than nominal or could raise appearance concerns.

In all cases, if a gift, hospitality, or benefit could create a perceived conflict, the staff member must disclose it and follow directions regarding acceptance, decline, or recusal.

## 8. Review, Recusal, and Approval Processes

### 8.1 Primary Points of Contact

Unless otherwise specified:

- For most staff, disclosures and questions should be directed to:
- The staff member’s**immediate supervisor**; and
- The**Executive Director**or the designated HR/Operations lead (if one exists).
- For the Executive Director’s own conflicts:
- Disclosures must be made to the**Board President**(or Board Chair) and, if appropriate, to the**Executive Committee**of the Board, consistent with the Board Conflict of Interest Policy and Executive Director scope and escalation documents.

The Organization may also designate an internal conflict of interest review contact (e.g., Operations & HR Manager) to coordinate documentation and follow-up.

### 8.2 Recusal Procedures for Staff

When a conflict arises (or may reasonably be perceived to arise), the staff member must:

1. **Disclose**the situation to the appropriate reviewer(s) as described in this Policy.
2. **Refrain**from participating in any related:
- Decision-making;
- Approval of contracts, invoices, proposals, or evaluations;
- Discussions intended to influence a decision.
3. **Step back**from roles, meetings, or communications related to the matter, unless expressly authorized to provide factual background and then recuse from any recommendation or decision.

Recusal expectations will be documented in writing (for example, via email or on the COI form) and may include:

- Assignment of the responsibility to another staff member.
- Alternative reviewer or approver for certain transactions.
- Restrictions on communications with vendors, partners, or other stakeholders on the specific topic.

### 8.3 Conflicts Involving the Executive Director

When a conflict involves the Executive Director:

- The Executive Director must disclose the conflict to the Board President (or Board Chair) as soon as reasonably practicable.
- The Board President or designated Board committee will:
- Review the disclosure.
- Determine appropriate management steps (including any recusal of the Executive Director from recommendations, negotiations, or approvals).
- Document the determination in Board or committee minutes or in another formal record, consistent with the Organization’s Board Conflict of Interest Policy.

Where staff are aware of a potential conflict involving the Executive Director, and do not feel comfortable raising it directly with the Executive Director, they may report it directly to the Board President under Section 9.

### 8.4 Approval and Management of Related‑Party or Conflicted Transactions

In situations where a transaction or relationship involving a conflict cannot reasonably be avoided and may still be in the Organization’s best interest (for example, a specialized service provider with unique qualifications), the Organization may proceed only if:

1. The conflict has been fully disclosed in writing.
2. The conflicted staff member has recused from decision-making.
3. An appropriate, unconflicted decision-maker (e.g., the Executive Director, Board President, or Board committee, depending on the matter) has:
- Considered whether alternative options are available on equal or better terms.
- Determined that the proposed arrangement is fair, reasonable, and in the Organization’s best interest.
4. The evaluation and decision are documented, including:
- The nature of the conflict.
- Alternatives considered.
- The basis for concluding the arrangement is fair and appropriate.
- The recusal of the conflicted staff member.

## 9. Reporting Concerns and Non‑Retaliation

### 9.1 Reporting Channels

Covered Staff who believe that:

- A conflict of interest has not been properly disclosed or managed; or
- They are being asked to engage in conduct that may violate this Policy or applicable law

must report their concerns promptly. Concerns may be raised through one or more of the following channels:

1. The staff member’s direct supervisor.
2. The Executive Director.
3. If the concern involves the Executive Director or the staff member is uncomfortable raising the issue through normal supervisory channels:
- The Board President (or Board Chair), in accordance with the Organization’s Board and staff escalation procedures.
4. Any designated hotline, reporting tool, or compliance contact specifically identified in the Organization’s Staff Code of Conduct or whistleblower policies.

Reports may be made in writing or orally. The Organization may request a written summary to aid investigation and recordkeeping.

### 9.2 Non‑Retaliation

The Organization strictly prohibits retaliation against any staff member who, in good faith:

- Makes a report or complaint about a potential conflict of interest or violation of this Policy.
- Participates in an investigation or review of a reported concern.
- Refuses to participate in conduct that they reasonably believe violates this Policy or applicable law.

“Good faith” means the staff member had a reasonable basis to believe the information reported was true, even if subsequent investigation shows no violation occurred.

Any staff member who engages in retaliation is subject to corrective action, up to and including termination of employment or termination of engagement for contractors.

## 10. Documentation and Recordkeeping

To ensure consistency, accountability, and alignment with the Organization’s Board Conflict of Interest Policy, the Organization will:

1. Maintain**signed Conflict of Interest Acknowledgement and Disclosure Forms**for all Covered Staff:
- From hiring or onboarding; and
- From each annual disclosure cycle.
2. Keep**written records of specific conflict reviews and determinations**, including:
- The nature of the conflict.
- Steps taken to investigate or evaluate options.
- Decisions made (approval, conditions, or prohibition).
- Recusal instructions and any management plans.
3. Maintain**records of training and communications**related to this Policy (e.g., attendance logs, training materials, policy distribution dates).
4. Protect these records as confidential personnel or governance records, disclosing them only to:
- Individuals with a legitimate, the Organization-related need to know; or
- External parties as required by law, regulation, audit, or regulatory inquiries.

Record retention periods will follow the Organization’s broader document retention and HR record policies.

## 11. Training and Integration With Other Policies

The Organization will integrate this Policy with other staff-facing policies and processes, including:

- Staff Code of Conduct.
- Confidentiality, privacy, and data-protection policies.
- Safeguarding and safety policies for children and families.
- Whistleblower and reporting policies.
- Employment agreements and contractor agreements.
- Executive Director Scope & Escalation guidelines.

Covered Staff may be required to participate in periodic training on conflicts of interest, ethics, confidentiality, and related topics. Completion of required trainings is a condition of ongoing employment or engagement.

## 12. Violations and Consequences

Failure to comply with this Policy—including failure to disclose, misrepresentation, or failure to follow recusal or management directions—may result in corrective or disciplinary action, up to and including:

- Verbal or written warning.
- Mandatory additional training or supervision.
- Reassignment of duties or changes to authority or access.
- Revocation of approval for outside employment or engagements.
- Suspension.
- Termination of employment or termination of contractor engagement.
- Referral to law enforcement or regulatory authorities, where appropriate.

Disciplinary decisions will consider:

- The nature and seriousness of the violation.
- Whether the violation was intentional, reckless, or negligent.
- Whether the staff member promptly self‑reported the conflict or concern.
- Potential or actual harm to the Organization, its community, or its reputation.

Nothing in this Policy alters the at‑will nature of employment (where applicable) or any rights and obligations under separate employment agreements or contracts.

## 13. Annual Acknowledgement and Short Disclosure Form

Each year, the Organization will provide a Staff Conflict of Interest Acknowledgement and Disclosure Form that:

1. Acknowledgement SectionRequires the staff member to:
- Confirm receipt of the current Staff Conflict of Interest Policy and understanding that it aligns with the Organization’s Board Conflict of Interest Policy.
- Acknowledge a duty to:
- Avoid conflicts of interest;
- Disclose actual, potential, and perceived conflicts;
- Recuse and follow guidance when conflicts arise;
- Update disclosures promptly if circumstances change.
2. Disclosure QuestionsAsks the staff member to:
- Indicate whether they (or a Related Party) currently:
- Have any financial interests, ownership, or leadership roles in organizations that do business with, seek to do business with, or receive funding from the Organization.
- Have any outside employment, consulting, or business activities that interact, or could reasonably interact, with the Organization programs, vendors, or donors.
- Have any family or close personal relationships with the Organization employees, contractors, or Board members where conflicts might arise.
- Either:
- Check “No, I have no reportable conflicts at this time”; or
- Check “Yes” and complete the short disclosure fields.
3. Short Disclosure FieldsFor each disclosed situation, the form provides space for:
- Name of the other organization or individual.
- Nature of the relationship (e.g., owner, employee, spouse employed, board member).
- Type of interaction with the Organization (e.g., vendor, grantee, partner, sponsor, contractor).
- Brief description of the actual or potential conflict.
- Any steps already being taken to mitigate or manage the situation.
4. Certification and Duty to UpdateRequires the staff member to certify that:
- The information provided is true, complete, and accurate to the best of their knowledge as of the date signed.
- They understand and agree that they must promptly notify the Organization in writing and update their disclosure if any information changes or if new potential conflicts arise.

The signed form will be retained in accordance with Section 10 of this Policy.

## 14. Questions and Interpretation

Staff are encouraged to seek guidance early and often. Questions about this Policy or about specific situations should be directed to:

- The staff member’s immediate supervisor; and/or
- The Executive Director or designated HR/Operations lead.

Questions involving the Executive Director’s own potential conflicts may be directed to the Board President.

In the event of any inconsistency between this Policy and the Organization’s Board Conflict of Interest Policy, the Board policy will govern Board processes; for staff matters, this Policy is intended to align with and complement Board standards. If there is uncertainty about how to apply one or both policies, the matter should be escalated to the Executive Director and/or Board President for clarification.

The Organization reserves the right to interpret, modify, or update this Policy at any time, consistent with applicable law and best practices.

## 15. Effective Date and Review

This the Organization Staff Conflict of Interest Policy (2026 Draft):

- Is intended to take effect upon formal approval by the the Organization Board of Directors or other authorized body, after consultation with the Executive Director and legal counsel as appropriate.
- Will be reviewed periodically—at least every three (3) years, or more frequently if laws, regulations, or the Organization’s structure or activities change in ways that warrant revision.
- Will be updated as needed to remain aligned with the Organization’s Board Conflict of Interest Policy, Staff Code of Conduct, and other organizational policies.

Upon final approval, the Organization will distribute the adopted Policy to all Covered Staff and implement the associated annual acknowledgement and short disclosure form described in this document.
