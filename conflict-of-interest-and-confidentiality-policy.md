# the Organization Conflict of Interest Policy (2026 Updated)

**[ORGANIZATION NAME] CONFLICT OF INTEREST AND CONFIDENTIALITY POLICY** [Revision date]

## Article I. Purpose, Policy, and Scope

### Section 1.01 Purpose

The purpose of this Conflict of Interest and Confidentiality Policy (the “Policy”) is to protect the interests of the [Organization Name] (the “Organization”) when it is contemplating entering into any transaction, relationship, or arrangement that might:

- provide a private benefit to a Covered Person (as defined below) or a Related Party;
- create an excess benefit transaction within the meaning of Section 4958 of the Internal Revenue Code; or
- otherwise compromise the Covered Person’s duty of loyalty to the Organization.

This Policy is intended to supplement, and not replace, any applicable federal or state laws or regulations governing conflicts of interest, whistleblowing, confidentiality, or governance of nonprofit organizations, as well as any provisions in the Organization’s Certificate of Incorporation and Bylaws.

### Section 1.02 Policy

The Organization’s directors, officers, employees, and volunteers occupy positions of trust and responsibility. Each Covered Person is expected to:

1. Act in good faith and in a manner the person reasonably believes to be in the best interests of the Organization.
2. Place the interests of the Organization above personal interests and avoid improper personal gain from their position.
3. Disclose fully and promptly any Financial Interest or other circumstances that might reasonably be perceived as a conflict of interest.
4. Refrain from influencing or participating in decisions when they have a conflict of interest, except as specifically permitted under this Policy.

The Organization will not enter into or continue any transaction or relationship with a Covered Person or a Related Party unless the transaction or relationship is:

- in the Organization’s best interest;
- fair and reasonable to the Organization; and
- approved in accordance with the procedures in this Policy.

### Section 1.03 Scope and Covered Persons

This Policy applies to all of the following individuals (collectively,**“Covered Persons”**):

1. Members of the Board of Directors of the Organization (“Directors” or “Board Members”);
2. Officers of the Organization (as defined in the Bylaws);
3. The Executive Director and all other Key Employees;
4. All employees of the Organization, whether full-time, part-time, temporary, or contract-based;
5. Volunteers who (a) serve in an ongoing leadership or advisory capacity, (b) regularly access Confidential Information, or (c) are specifically asked to sign this Policy; and
6. Any other person or class of persons designated by the Board of Directors as a Covered Person for purposes of this Policy.

The Board (or a committee it designates) may determine that additional roles (e.g., long-term contractors, consultants, or committee members) must be treated as Covered Persons based on their responsibilities.

## Article II. Definitions

For purposes of this Policy, the following definitions apply:

### Section 2.01 Confidential Information

“**Confidential Information**” means non-public information regarding:

1. The Organization’s business and affairs, including but not limited to historical financial statements, budgets, financial projections, capital plans, internal policies, and internal strategy documents;
2. Past, current, or specifically identified prospective sources of financial or in-kind support for the Organization (e.g., donors, sponsors, foundation and government funders, corporate partners);
3. Past, current, or specifically identified prospective recipients of financial, programmatic, or other support from the Organization (e.g., grantees, scholarship recipients, partner organizations);
4. Past, current, or specifically identified prospective employees, contractors, or volunteers when personnel information is not publicly available;
5. Any personally identifiable information or sensitive data about the Organization community members, including but not limited to children, families, volunteers, or program participants; and
6. Any pending, threatened, or reasonably anticipated investigations, claims, or litigation involving the Organization.

Confidential Information does**not**include information that (a) becomes publicly available through no wrongful act or omission of the Covered Person, or (b) is lawfully obtained by the Covered Person from a third party not under an obligation of confidentiality to the Organization.

### Section 2.02 Financial Interest

A Covered Person has a “**Financial Interest**” if the person has, directly or indirectly, through business, investment, or family:

1. An ownership or investment interest in any entity with which the Organization has, or is negotiating, a transaction or arrangement;
2. A compensation arrangement with the Organization or with any entity or individual with which the Organization has, or is negotiating, a transaction or arrangement. “Compensation” includes direct and indirect remuneration, honoraria, consulting fees, commissions, gifts, or favors that are more than nominal in nature;
3. A potential ownership or investment interest in, or compensation arrangement with, any entity or individual with which the Organization is considering a transaction or arrangement; or
4. A legal commitment, fiduciary duty, or financial obligation (including, for example, service as a director, trustee, officer, employee, or key volunteer) to act in the interests of another entity or individual whose interests may conflict with the Organization’s interests in a particular matter.

A Financial Interest is not necessarily a conflict of interest. Under this Policy, a Financial Interest becomes a**conflict of interest**when the Board or its designated committee determines, after full disclosure of all relevant facts, that the Financial Interest could reasonably be expected to impair the Covered Person’s objectivity or independent judgment.

### Section 2.03 Related Party and Related-Party Transaction

1. A “Related Party” includes any of the following: a. Any Director, officer, employee, Key Employee, or volunteer of the Organization or its affiliates;b. Any Relative of any person in subsection (a). “Relative” means a spouse or domestic partner, ancestor, sibling or half-sibling, child, grandchild, great-grandchild, and spouses or domestic partners of any of the foregoing;c. Any entity or trust of which a person described in (a) or (b) serves as a director, trustee, officer, employee, or key volunteer;d. Any entity or trust in which a person described in (a) or (b) owns or beneficially holds a 35% or greater ownership or beneficial interest;e. Any partnership or professional corporation in which a person described in (a) or (b) has a direct or indirect ownership interest in excess of 5%; orf. Any other entity or trust in which a person described in (a) or (b) has a material financial interest as determined by the Board or its designated committee.
2. A “Related-Party Transaction” means any transaction, arrangement, or relationship (including compensation arrangements and transfers of goods or services) between the Organization and:
- a Related Party; or
- any other entity or individual in which a Related Party has a Financial Interest that could reasonably be affected by the transaction.

### Section 2.04 Interested Person

An “**Interested Person**” is any Covered Person who has a direct or indirect Financial Interest in a matter under consideration by the Board or a Board committee.

### Section 2.05 Key Employee

A “**Key Employee**” is any employee (other than a Director or officer) who:

1. Receives compensation from the Organization and all related organizations in excess of \$25,000 for the calendar year ending within the Organization’s fiscal year; and
2. Meets at least one of the following: a. Has responsibilities, powers, or influence over the Organization as a whole that is similar to those of an officer or director;b. Manages a discrete segment or activity of the Organization that represents 10% or more of the Organization’s activities, assets, income, or expenses; orc. Has or shares authority to control or determine 10% or more of the Organization’s capital expenditures, operating budget, or compensation for employees.

### Section 2.06 Independent Director

An “**Independent Director**” is a Director who:

1. Is not, and has not been within the last three (3) years, an employee of the Organization or any of its affiliates;
2. Does not receive, directly or indirectly, any compensation or other financial benefit from the Organization, other than reasonable reimbursement of expenses or de minimis benefits available to all Directors on the same terms; and
3. Is not a Related Party to any person who receives significant compensation or material financial benefits from the Organization, except as permitted by applicable law.

### Section 2.07 Excess Benefit Transaction

An “**Excess Benefit Transaction**” is any transaction in which the Organization provides an economic benefit to or for the use of a Covered Person or other “disqualified person” (as defined by the Internal Revenue Code) and the value of the benefit exceeds the value of the consideration received by the Organization.

## Article III. General Standards of Conduct and Conflicts of Interest

### Section 3.01 Duty of Loyalty and General Standards

All Covered Persons owe a duty of loyalty to the Organization. Covered Persons must:

1. Act in the Organization’s best interests, not for personal gain or the benefit of a Related Party;
2. Avoid activities or relationships that materially interfere with their ability to act in the Organization’s best interests;
3. Use the Organization’s resources (including time, funds, equipment, intellectual property, data, and Confidential Information) only for authorized organizational purposes; and
4. Promptly disclose any actual, potential, or perceived conflicts of interest as required under this Policy.

### Section 3.02 Gifts, Favors, and Other Benefits

Covered Persons may not solicit or accept personal gifts, favors, loans, or other benefits from any individual or entity that does, or seeks to do, business with the Organization, if the gift or benefit:

- is more than nominal in value;
- could reasonably be perceived as intended to influence the Covered Person’s judgment or actions; or
- could create a sense of obligation to the donor that is inconsistent with the Organization’s interests.

Modest items such as reasonable meals in the course of business, small tokens of appreciation, or promotional items of minimal value are generally permissible, provided they do not influence or appear to influence decision-making.

### Section 3.03 Outside Positions and Activities

Covered Persons may engage in outside employment, consulting, board service, or other activities, provided that:

1. Such activities do not interfere with their responsibilities to the Organization;
2. The Covered Person discloses any Financial Interest or potential conflict arising from the outside activity; and
3. The Covered Person does not use the Organization’s name, mark, resources, or Confidential Information for personal gain or to benefit an outside organization, except as explicitly authorized.

## Article IV. Conflict Disclosure, Review, and Committee Procedures

### Section 4.01 Conflict of Interest Review Committee

1. The Board shall designate a standing committee (the “COI Committee”) to administer and oversee this Policy. Unless otherwise determined by the Board, the Compensation Committee shall serve as the COI Committee. The Board may also expand the scope of another standing committee (e.g., Finance or Audit, if created in the Bylaws) to serve in this role.
2. The COI Committee shall consist solely of Independent Directors. The Board may invite non-voting advisors (e.g., legal counsel, external accountants, or subject-matter experts) to assist the COI Committee.
3. The COI Committee is responsible for:
- reviewing annual and interim disclosures;
- making determinations as to whether a conflict of interest or Related-Party Transaction exists;
- overseeing the procedures described in this Policy;
- recommending appropriate actions or safeguards to the Board; and
- ensuring that minutes and documentation required by this Policy are maintained.

### Section 4.02 Duty to Disclose

1. Initial and Annual Disclosure. Each Covered Person must complete and sign the Annual Conflict of Interest and Confidentiality Disclosure Form attached as Appendix B:
- upon initial engagement or appointment as a Covered Person; and
- annually thereafter, by the deadline established by the COI Committee (generally no later than the first Board meeting of each calendar year).
2. Ongoing Disclosure. Covered Persons must promptly update their disclosure form and notify the Executive Director or the COI Committee Chair if:
- any new Financial Interest arises;
- any existing Financial Interest changes in a material way; or
- they become aware of any new situation or transaction that may give rise to a conflict of interest.
3. Executive Director-Specific Reporting.
- The**Executive Director**must report all actual, potential, or perceived conflicts of interest (including those involving Related Parties) directly to the**President of the Board**and the**COI Committee Chair**, in writing, as soon as practicable after becoming aware of the matter.
- Staff, volunteers, or contractors who become aware of a possible conflict involving the Executive Director may report concerns to (i) the Board President, (ii) the COI Committee Chair, or (iii) the officer designated in the Organization’s whistleblower or escalation policies.

### Section 4.03 Determining Whether a Conflict Exists

1. After full disclosure of the Financial Interest and all material facts, the Interested Person may address the Board or COI Committee to explain the circumstances.
2. After any such presentation, the Interested Person must leave the meeting room (physical or virtual) while the Board or COI Committee discusses and votes on whether a conflict of interest exists.
3. Only disinterested, Independent Directors may vote on whether a conflict exists. A simple majority of disinterested, Independent Directors present shall determine whether a conflict exists, unless a higher standard is required by law or the Organization’s Bylaws.

### Section 4.04 Quorum and Recusal Rules

1. For purposes of Board or COI Committee action on a matter involving a conflict of interest or Related-Party Transaction:
- **Quorum**is determined**without counting**any Interested Person as present for that specific agenda item.
- A quorum is present if a majority of the**disinterested**Directors or committee members are present, consistent with the Bylaws and applicable law.
2. An Interested Person:
- May provide factual information or respond to questions at the request of the Board or COI Committee;
- Must**recuse**themself from the deliberation and**shall not vote**on the matter; and
- Shall not attempt to influence, directly or indirectly, the deliberation or vote of other Directors or committee members regarding the matter.
3. If, after excluding all Interested Persons, a quorum of disinterested Directors or committee members cannot be achieved, the Board must either:
- defer the matter until sufficient Independent Directors are available; or
- seek advice of independent counsel or other outside experts and follow applicable legal requirements for approval of conflicted transactions.

### Section 4.05 Procedures for Addressing a Conflict or Related-Party Transaction

If a conflict of interest or Related-Party Transaction is identified, the Board or COI Committee shall:

1. Determine whether the matter can, consistent with law and the Organization’s best interests, proceed under appropriate safeguards.
2. Where appropriate, appoint a disinterested person or subcommittee to research alternatives to the proposed transaction or arrangement.
3. After exercising due diligence, determine whether the Organization can obtain a more advantageous transaction or arrangement from a person or entity that would not give rise to a conflict of interest.
4. If a more advantageous alternative is not reasonably available under circumstances that do not give rise to a conflict, determine whether the transaction or arrangement:
- is in the Organization’s best interests;
- is fair and reasonable to the Organization; and
- is consistent with the Organization’s tax-exempt status and mission.
5. Approve or reject the transaction or arrangement by a majority vote of the disinterested Directors or committee members.
6. Ensure that the decision, the basis for the decision, and any conditions or safeguards are documented in the minutes and/or in a written resolution, consistent with the Related-Party Transaction Approval Checklist (Appendix A).

### Section 4.06 Violations of the Policy

1. If the Board or COI Committee has reasonable cause to believe that a Covered Person has failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict of interest or has otherwise violated this Policy, the Chair shall:
- inform the Covered Person of the basis for such belief; and
- provide the Covered Person an opportunity to explain the alleged failure or violation.
2. After hearing the Covered Person’s response and making any further investigation deemed necessary, the Board or COI Committee shall determine whether a violation has occurred.
3. If a violation is found, the Organization may take appropriate disciplinary and corrective action, which may include, without limitation:
- written warning or counseling;
- additional training;
- reassignment of responsibilities;
- termination of employment or volunteer service;
- removal from the Board, consistent with the Bylaws; and/or
- remedial measures to address any improper transaction (including rescission or modification).

## Article V. Compensation Review and Approval

### Section 5.01 Scope

This Article applies to:

- compensation and benefits for the Executive Director;
- compensation and benefits for officers and Key Employees; and
- any other compensation arrangements that the Board or COI Committee determines present potential conflict-of-interest or excess-benefit concerns.

### Section 5.02 Independent Review and Approval

1. Compensation for the Executive Director, officers, and Key Employees must be reviewed and approved by a body of Independent Directors (which may be the Compensation Committee, the Finance Committee, or another Board committee, as designated by the Board).
2. Any Director who is receiving, or whose Related Party is receiving, compensation from the Organization for services must**not**participate in discussions or vote on matters pertaining to that person’s compensation, but may provide factual information at the request of the Board or committee.

### Section 5.03 Use of Comparability Data

In establishing or reviewing compensation for the Executive Director, officers, and Key Employees, the reviewing body shall obtain and consider appropriate comparability data, which may include:

1. Compensation paid by similarly situated organizations (in terms of size, budget, geography, and mission) for functionally comparable positions;
2. Prior compensation levels for the same or similar roles within the Organization, adjusted for current scope and responsibilities;
3. Written offers from competing organizations, if available; and
4. Independent compensation surveys or written expert compensation studies.

### Section 5.04 Documentation Requirements

The reviewing body shall contemporaneously document in its minutes or written resolutions:

1. The names and roles of individuals present for discussions and votes on compensation matters;
2. The comparability data and other information reviewed;
3. Any material terms of the compensation package (including salary, bonus or incentive plans, benefits, perquisites, and severance terms);
4. Any disclosures of conflicts of interest and the steps taken to address them, including recusal; and
5. The final decision, including the date of approval and the voting results.

### Section 5.05 Executive Director Conflicts Related to Compensation

1. The Executive Director may present information, market data, or recommendations regarding compensation for other staff but must not participate in deliberations or votes on their own compensation or on any arrangement in which they are an Interested Person.
2. Any conflict of interest involving the Executive Director’s compensation or benefits must be reported and handled pursuant to Section 4.02 and reviewed by Independent Directors who are not Related Parties to the Executive Director.

## Article VI. Confidentiality and Permitted Disclosures

### Section 6.01 General Confidentiality Obligations

Each Covered Person acknowledges the confidential and proprietary nature of the Organization’s Confidential Information. Each Covered Person agrees that they will:

1. Use Confidential Information solely in furtherance of the Organization’s mission and lawful purposes;
2. Not disclose Confidential Information to any person or entity outside the Organization except as:
- expressly authorized by the Board, the Executive Director, or another authorized officer;
- required to carry out the Organization’s programs or operations consistent with this Policy and applicable law; or
- permitted under Section 6.03 (whistleblower and regulatory carveouts);
3. Safeguard Confidential Information against loss, unauthorized use, or disclosure, including when using digital tools, remote work arrangements, or third-party platforms; and
4. Return or securely destroy the Organization’s Confidential Information upon termination of their role, or at any time upon request, subject to legal retention requirements.

### Section 6.02 Existing Exceptions

The confidentiality obligations in this Policy do not apply to information that the Covered Person demonstrates:

1. Was generally available to the public prior to, and other than as a result of, disclosure by the Covered Person;
2. Was available to the Covered Person on a non-confidential basis prior to its disclosure in connection with their the Organization role; or
3. Is required to be disclosed by law, subpoena, court order, or valid governmental or regulatory request, provided that the Covered Person (to the extent permitted by law) promptly notifies the Organization’s Executive Director or Board President so that the Organization may seek protective measures as appropriate.

### Section 6.03 Whistleblower, Regulatory, and Reporting Carveouts

Nothing in this Policy is intended to, and**nothing in this Policy shall be interpreted to**:

1. Prohibit or restrict any Covered Person from reporting in good faith: to the Organization’s designated whistleblower channels, to the Organization’s legal counsel, or to a governmental, regulatory, law-enforcement, or oversight body (such as the Internal Revenue Service, a state Attorney General, the U.S. Department of Labor, or equivalent agencies), as permitted or protected by law.
- suspected violations of law;
- suspected financial improprieties, fraud, or misuse of charitable assets;
- concerns about child safety, safeguarding, harassment, discrimination, or retaliation; or
- suspected violations of the Organization policies;
2. Require prior authorization from the Organization before making a good-faith report to a regulatory or law-enforcement agency or participating in an investigation by such an agency.
3. Prohibit a Covered Person from seeking legal advice regarding their rights under applicable whistleblower, anti-retaliation, or employment laws.
4. Prevent any Covered Person from disclosing information as otherwise required or expressly protected by law (including but not limited to laws protecting whistleblowers, the rights of employees to discuss terms and conditions of employment, and laws governing reports of child abuse or neglect).

Covered Persons who disclose Confidential Information in good faith as part of a protected whistleblower or regulatory report will not be deemed to have violated this Policy solely by virtue of such disclosure.

### Section 6.04 Violations of Confidentiality

If the Board or COI Committee has reasonable cause to believe that a Covered Person has violated their obligations with respect to Confidential Information (outside of the permitted whistleblower or legal disclosures described above), the Board or COI Committee shall:

1. Inform the Covered Person of the basis for such belief;
2. Provide the Covered Person an opportunity to respond; and
3. After any further investigation deemed necessary, determine whether a violation has occurred and, if so, take appropriate corrective or disciplinary action, which may include:
- additional training or counseling;
- restrictions on access to certain categories of information;
- termination of employment or volunteer service;
- removal from the Board, in accordance with the Bylaws; and/or
- legal action, where warranted.

## Article VII. Records of Proceedings and Documentation

### Section 7.01 Minutes and Written Records

Minutes of Board and relevant committee meetings must include:

1. The names of the persons who disclosed or were otherwise identified as having a Financial Interest or being an Interested Person;
2. The nature of the Financial Interest and any related facts;
3. A summary of any presentation or information provided by the Interested Person;
4. A record that the Interested Person left the meeting during deliberations and voting on the matter;
5. The names of all persons present for the discussion and vote, and their voting status (e.g., voting, recused, non-voting advisor);
6. A description of the deliberations, including any alternatives considered; and
7. The decision reached (approved, rejected, deferred, or approved with conditions), the vote count, and any restrictions or safeguards imposed.

### Section 7.02 Retention of Documentation

The Organization shall maintain:

1. Signed annual and interim disclosure forms for all Covered Persons;
2. Minutes and written resolutions relating to conflict-of-interest determinations, Related-Party Transactions, and compensation decisions covered by this Policy; and
3. Supporting documentation, including comparability data and the materials referenced in the**Related-Party Transaction Approval Checklist**.

These records shall be retained in accordance with the Organization’s document retention policies and applicable law.

## Article VIII. Annual Acknowledgements and Training

### Section 8.01 Annual Acknowledgement

Each Covered Person must, at least annually:

1. Receive or have access to a current copy of this Policy;
2. Review and acknowledge in writing that they have read and understand the Policy;
3. Disclose any current and prior (previously unreported) conflicts of interest or Related-Party Transactions; and
4. Affirm their commitment to comply with this Policy and any related the Organization procedures.

The updated**Annual Conflict of Interest and Confidentiality Disclosure Form**(Appendix B) shall be used for this purpose.

### Section 8.02 Training and Orientation

The Organization will incorporate this Policy into:

1. Board orientation, including for newly elected or appointed Directors and officers;
2. Onboarding for the Executive Director, Key Employees, and staff in roles with significant financial, contractual, or programmatic authority; and
3. Periodic refresher training, particularly when the Policy is revised or when the Organization’s operations, risk profile, or regulatory environment significantly changes.

## Article IX. Periodic Reviews and Use of Outside Experts

### Section 9.01 Periodic Reviews

To ensure that the Organization operates in a manner consistent with its charitable purposes and does not engage in activities that could jeopardize its reputation or tax-exempt status, the Organization shall conduct periodic reviews, which shall at minimum consider:

1. Whether compensation arrangements and benefits are:
- reasonable;
- based on competent survey or comparability information; and
- the result of independent, arm’s-length bargaining; and
2. Whether partnerships, joint ventures, management agreements, sponsorships, and other business arrangements:
- conform to the Organization’s written policies;
- are properly authorized and documented;
- reflect reasonable payments for goods and services;
- further the Organization’s charitable purposes; and
- do not result in inurement, impermissible private benefit, or excess benefit transactions.

### Section 9.02 Use of Outside Experts

In conducting conflict-of-interest determinations, compensation reviews, or periodic reviews, the Organization may, but is not required to, engage outside advisors (e.g., legal counsel, accountants, or compensation consultants). Use of such experts does not relieve the Board of its fiduciary duties to review, understand, and make informed decisions regarding the matters at issue.

## Article X. Amendment and Effective Date

### Section 10.01 Amendment

The Board of Directors may amend, modify, or repeal this Policy by an affirmative vote as required under the Bylaws. Any amendments shall be documented in the Board minutes and distributed to all Covered Persons in a timely manner.

### Section 10.02 Effective Date

This Policy is**effective January 1, 2026**and supersedes the the Organization Conflict of Interest Policy last revised in January 2024.

# Appendix A: Related-Party Transaction Approval Checklist

The following checklist is intended to guide the Board or COI Committee when reviewing any proposed or existing Related-Party Transaction. It should be adapted and expanded as needed for the specific circumstances.

For each proposed Related-Party Transaction, the Board or COI Committee should:

1. Identify the Parties and Relationship
- Identify the Related Party (individual or entity).
- Describe the relationship between the Related Party and the Organization (e.g., Director’s spouse owns a business that will provide services).
- Identify all Covered Persons who are Interested Persons in connection with the transaction.
2. Describe the Transaction
- Describe the material terms of the transaction (goods or services to be provided, price, duration, renewal terms, exclusivity, and any termination provisions).
- Identify whether the transaction is new, a renewal, or a modification of an existing relationship.
3. Obtain and Review Disclosures
- Confirm that the Interested Person(s) have disclosed all relevant Financial Interests and relationships.
- Confirm that any related disclosures have been documented on the Annual or Interim Disclosure Form.
4. Assess Conflicts and Recusal
- Determine whether the Financial Interest constitutes a conflict of interest under this Policy.
- Ensure that all Interested Persons have recused themselves from deliberation and voting and are not counted toward quorum for this matter.
5. Gather Comparability and Market Data (as applicable)
- Obtain written bids, proposals, or quotes from independent vendors, where feasible.
- Review recent or comparable transactions with independent parties for similar goods or services.
- Consider industry benchmarks, published rate information, or other objective comparability data.
6. Evaluate Fairness and Charitable Purpose
- Assess whether the transaction is fair and reasonable to the Organization, considering price, quality, service, risk, and mission alignment.
- Confirm that the transaction furthers the Organization’s charitable purposes and does not primarily benefit the Related Party.
- Evaluate whether the transaction presents any risk of inurement, impermissible private benefit, or excess benefit transaction.
7. Consider Alternatives
- Determine whether reasonably available alternatives (e.g., other vendors, different structures, or internal options) could provide similar benefits to the Organization at equal or lower cost and risk.
- Document the alternatives considered and the reasons they were not selected.
8. Decision and Conditions
- Vote on whether to approve, reject, or modify the proposed transaction.
- If approved, consider any conditions or safeguards (e.g., shorter term, periodic re-bidding, reporting requirements, performance measures, or enhanced monitoring).
9. Documentation
- Record in the minutes:
- The names of all persons present and their roles (including Interested Persons and independent advisors);
- The materials reviewed and data relied upon;
- The discussion of alternatives;
- The final decision and vote (including any recusals); and
- Any conditions placed on the approval.
10. Ongoing Monitoring
- Designate a responsible individual or committee to monitor the performance of the transaction, compliance with its terms, and any changes in circumstances that may affect the conflict analysis.
- Revisit the transaction periodically, especially upon renewal or significant modification, using this checklist.
