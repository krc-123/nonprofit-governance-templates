**3.6 Whistleblower Policy – Staff and Contractors**

1.  **Background and Objectives**

    1.  The [Organization Name] Code of Conduct requires directors, volunteers, contractors, and employees to observe high standards of business and personal ethics in the conduct of their duties and responsibilities. Everyone acting for or on behalf of the Organization must practice honesty and integrity, follow the Organization policies, and comply with all applicable laws and regulations.

    2.  The objectives of this Whistleblower Policy are to explain, in plain language, how the Organization staff and contractors can:

        1.  Raise concerns about suspected violations of law, serious misconduct, misuse of the Organization resources, health or safety risks, discrimination or harassment, financial impropriety, or other violations of the Organization policies, including on a confidential or anonymous basis;

        2.  Ensure that concerns are received, recorded, reviewed, and addressed in a consistent, fair, and timely way;

        3.  Protect directors, volunteers, contractors, and employees who report concerns in good faith (“whistleblowers”) from retaliatory actions.

2.  **Reporting Responsibility**

    1.  Each director, volunteer, contractor, and employee of the [Organization Name] has a responsibility to speak up and report, in accordance with this Whistleblower Policy, any serious concern about the Organization’s operations, people, or use of resources.

        1.  (a) Any suspected violation of law or government regulation, including fraud, theft, embezzlement, discrimination, harassment, child protection concerns, or health and safety risks;

        2.  (b) Questionable or improper accounting, financial reporting, or handling of the Organization funds or property, including suspected fraud, misrepresentation, or concealment of financial information;

        3.  (c) Violations or suspected violations of the the Organization Code of Conduct or other the Organization policies that are serious in nature (collectively referred to in this Policy as “Concerns”).

3.  **Authority of Executive Director**

    1.  Except as otherwise provided in this Policy (for example, when a Concern involves the Executive Director), all reported Concerns will be forwarded to the Executive Director in accordance with the procedures set forth herein.

    2.  The Executive Director will oversee or conduct an appropriate review or investigation of all reported Concerns delegated to the Executive Director under this Policy and will make appropriate recommendations regarding any corrective or disciplinary action.

4.  **No Retaliation**

    1.  This Whistleblower Policy is intended to encourage and enable directors, volunteers, contractors, and employees to raise Concerns within the Organization for investigation and appropriate action.

    2.  No director, volunteer, contractor, or employee who, in good faith, reports a Concern or participates in an investigation shall be subject to intimidation, threats, harassment, retaliation, or, in the case of an employee, adverse employment consequences (such as demotion, reduction in pay, or termination) because of that report or participation.

    3.  Any director, volunteer, contractor, or employee who retaliates against someone who has reported a Concern in good faith or has participated in an investigation is subject to corrective action, which may include removal from a volunteer role, termination of employment, or non-renewal or termination of a contract.

5.  **Reporting Concerns**

    1.  Employees, Contractors, Directors, and Volunteers

        1.  Employees, contractors, directors, and volunteers may report Concerns to the Executive Director by email, in writing (letter), or in person. The Organization encourages reporters to provide as much detail as possible (who, what, when, where, and any documents or other evidence) to allow for an effective review.

        2.  If an individual is uncomfortable reporting a Concern to the Executive Director, or if the Executive Director is the subject of the Concern or may have a conflict of interest, the individual should report the Concern directly to the President of the Board of Directors.

        3.  If a Concern is reported verbally, the Executive Director or the President of the Board of Directors, as applicable, will create a written record of the Concern to ensure a clear and accurate understanding of the issues raised.

        4.  Anonymous Concerns may be submitted in writing to the Executive Director or, if the Concern involves the Executive Director, to the President of the Board of Directors. Anonymous reports should include enough detail and supporting information to allow the Organization to evaluate and investigate the Concern. Anonymous reporting may limit the Organization’s ability to follow up with questions or to provide updates.

    2.  The Executive Director will address all reported Concerns that are not required under this Policy to be handled directly by the President of the Board of Directors.

        1.  The Organization will strive to acknowledge receipt of a written Concern within thirty (30) business days, if contact information is provided, either from the Executive Director or, if applicable, from the President of the Board of Directors.

        2.  The Organization will not be able to acknowledge receipt of anonymously submitted Concerns unless the reporter has provided a confidential way for the Organization to respond.

        3.  All reports will be reviewed promptly. The Executive Director (or, if applicable, the President of the Board of Directors) will determine the scope and method of the investigation and ensure that appropriate corrective or disciplinary action is recommended if the Concern is substantiated.

        4.  Action taken on a Concern will include a conclusion or other determination and, when feasible and appropriate, follow-up with the complainant to provide general information about the outcome or next steps, while respecting confidentiality and privacy obligations.

        5.  If a Concern is submitted anonymously and no safe way to contact the reporter is provided, the Organization may not be able to give the reporter any follow-up information about the Concern.

        6.  The Executive Director, or the President of the Board of Directors when leading or overseeing a review, has the authority to retain outside legal counsel, accountants, investigators, or other resources deemed necessary to conduct a full and fair investigation of any Concern.

6.  **Acting in Good Faith**

    1.  Anyone reporting a Concern must act in good faith. This means the reporter must believe the information they are providing is true and that the situation described may show a violation of law, the Organization policy, or this Policy. The reporter is not required to be certain or to have proof, but should have reasonable grounds for the Concern and should provide information honestly.

    2.  Knowingly making a false report, or making allegations maliciously or with reckless disregard for the truth, is a serious violation of this Policy. Such conduct may result in corrective or disciplinary action, up to and including dismissal from a volunteer position, termination of employment, or non-renewal or termination of a contract, and may also give rise to other actions, including civil lawsuits.

7.  **Confidentiality**

    1.  Reports of Concerns, and investigations related to them, will be kept as confidential as is reasonably possible, consistent with the need to conduct a thorough and fair investigation, to protect individuals from harm, and to comply with applicable laws and legal processes. This confidentiality obligation applies to staff, contractors, and applicable Board members involved in handling or reviewing Concerns.

    2.  Information about a Concern will be shared only with those who have a legitimate need to know it in order to carry out their duties under this Policy or applicable law. Unnecessary or unauthorized disclosure of information about a Concern or an investigation to individuals not involved in the process is a serious offense and may result in discipline, up to and including termination of employment or non-renewal or termination of a contract, and may also give rise to other actions, including civil lawsuits.

## Emergency vs. Non-Emergency Concerns

An "emergency" Concern is a situation where there is an immediate risk of serious harm to a child, staff member, contractor, volunteer, or member of the public (for example, serious threats of violence, self-harm, or other immediate safety risks).

In an emergency, call 911 or local emergency services first. If the Concern involves possible child abuse or neglect, follow any required mandatory reporting obligations under applicable law. Once immediate safety and legal reporting needs have been addressed, the Concern should also be reported under this Whistleblower Policy.

Non-emergency Concerns are issues that are serious but do not present an immediate threat to health or safety. These should be reported as described in the "Reporting Concerns" section of this Policy.

## Anonymity

Reporters may choose to identify themselves or to report anonymously. Providing your name and contact information often helps the Organization investigate more effectively and to follow up with you, but the Organization understands that some people may feel safer reporting anonymously. Whether or not you identify yourself, the Organization will handle your report as described in this Policy.

## Investigation Process and Timelines

After a Concern is received, the Executive Director or the President of the Board of Directors, as applicable, will: (a) review the information to decide how serious and urgent the Concern is; (b) determine who should lead the review or investigation and whether outside experts are needed; (c) gather relevant information, which may include interviews and review of documents; and (d) reach a conclusion and determine any corrective or disciplinary action.

The Organization will normally begin reviewing a Concern within ten (10) business days of receipt. Many matters can be addressed within sixty (60) days, but more complex issues may take longer. Time frames may be extended based on the nature of the Concern, ongoing legal or regulatory processes, or other legitimate reasons. Where feasible, the Organization will provide general status updates to the reporter.

## Protection of Reporters

In addition to the "No Retaliation" section, the Organization will take appropriate steps to protect reporters from negative treatment because they raised a Concern or participated in an investigation. This includes protection from termination, demotion, loss of hours or pay, threats, harassment, or other actions that would discourage a reasonable person from speaking up.

Where appropriate, the Organization may put temporary measures in place during an investigation to help protect the reporter or other individuals (for example, changes in supervision or work assignments, interim no-contact expectations, or other reasonable steps), while seeking to avoid any impact that could itself feel retaliatory.

## Escalation Path When a Supervisor or the Executive Director Is Involved

If your Concern involves your direct supervisor, or you are not comfortable raising the issue with that supervisor, you may report your Concern directly to the Executive Director or, if appropriate, to another member of management designated by the Organization.

If your Concern involves the Executive Director or you believe the Executive Director may have a conflict of interest in addressing the Concern, you should report the Concern directly to the President of the Board of Directors. In such cases, the President of the Board of Directors will determine how the Concern will be reviewed or investigated, and whether the full Board or a committee of the Board should be involved.
