# the Organization Post-Award Donor Retention SOP (2026 Compliance)

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) is to:

- Guide the Organization staff in retaining and stewarding donors after a grant, sponsorship, or major gift is awarded.
- Ensure all post-award donor communications and stewardship activities:
- Comply with the Organization bylaws and codes of conduct.
- Maintain the Organization’s 501(c)(3) tax-exempt status.
- Protect donor and beneficiary privacy and confidentiality.
- Honor grant and gift restrictions, reporting requirements, and intellectual property terms.
- Accurately represent the Organization’s work, impact, and commitments.

This SOP is designed to be practical and actionable for Development, Programs, Communications, and Finance staff who interact with donors after an award is made.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to:

- All**post-award**activities following a written or verbal commitment of support, including:
- Foundation and corporate grants.
- Sponsorships and cause-marketing agreements.
- Major gifts and restricted gifts from individuals or family foundations.
- Multi-year pledges and renewal discussions.
- All**The Organization employees, contractors, volunteers, and board members**involved in:
- Donor acknowledgements and recognition (public and private).
- Stewardship communications, reports, and impact stories.
- Events, site visits, and media involving donors.
- Use of donor names, logos, and brand elements.
- Program or financial reporting to donors.

This SOP supplements, and does not replace, the the Organization Bylaws, Conflict of Interest Policy, Codes of Conduct, and Staff/Board NDA & Confidentiality Agreements. If there is any conflict, the Bylaws and Board-approved policies control.

## 3. Definitions

- **Donor**: Any individual, foundation, corporation, civic group, or other entity that provides financial support, in-kind gifts, or sponsorships to the Organization.
- **Post-award**: The period beginning when a donor has formally committed support (grant award letter, signed agreement, email confirmation, or recorded verbal commitment) and continuing through all stewardship, reporting, and renewal efforts.
- **Restricted funds**: Donations or grants that must be used for specific purposes, programs, populations, or time periods as defined in a grant agreement, gift letter, or donor correspondence.
- **Unrestricted funds**: Donations that the Organization may allocate at its discretion within the bounds of its mission and 501(c)(3) purposes.
- **Stewardship communication**: Any thank-you message, update, report, proposal for renewal, recognition, or impact story shared with donors after an award is made.
- **Personally Identifiable Information (PII)**: Any information that can be used to identify a person (e.g., name, address, email, phone, images, video, unique story details) especially of children and families.
- **Minor**: An individual under 18 years of age.
- **Press/media**: Public-facing content including press releases, website posts, newsletters, email campaigns, social media, conference presentations, or marketing materials.
- **Endorsement**: Any statement or implication that the Organization approves, promotes, or recommends a product, service, or political candidate/party.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

### 4.1 Board of Directors

- Upholds overall fiduciary, legal, and ethical oversight.
- Approves major institutional policies and positions that may appear in donor communications.
- Reviews and approves any significant naming opportunities or strategic corporate partnerships where reputational or compliance risk is high.

### 4.2 Executive Director (ED)

- Serves as the ultimate approver for:
- New or significantly revised donor recognition frameworks and naming opportunities.
- High-risk or sensitive donor communications (e.g., large corporate partnerships, complex restricted gifts, unusual publicity).
- Ensures staff follow this SOP and other the Organization policies.
- Escalates legal questions to counsel as needed (e.g., complex IP, media releases, or political/lobbying issues).

### 4.3 Development / Institutional Giving (e.g., Director of Development, Grants Manager, Donor Stewardship & Events Manager)

- Primary owners of post-award donor retention and stewardship.
- Ensure:
- Required acknowledgement letters are sent timely and accurately.
- Donor restrictions, recognition preferences, and reporting deadlines are recorded correctly in the CRM.
- All donor-facing messages are accurate and compliant with this SOP.
- Initiate and coordinate the approval workflow before public recognition, use of logos, or complex communications.

### 4.4 Programs / Program Managers

- Confirm feasibility and accuracy of program-related statements in donor materials.
- Track implementation and outcomes of grant-funded activities.
- Provide data, stories, and impact updates that are accurate and appropriately de-identified or consented.

### 4.5 Finance / Controller / Bookkeeper

- Track restricted and unrestricted funds in accordance with GAAP and the Organization policies.
- Confirm that use of funds aligns with donor restrictions.
- Provide financial data for reports and ensure consistency between donor reports and the Organization financial records.
- Coordinate with Development to flag any risk of noncompliance with grant budgets or spending timelines.

### 4.6 Communications / Marketing / Social Media

- Draft and disseminate donor-related public content only after:
- Required permissions are documented for names, logos, and quotes.
- Privacy and media release requirements (especially for minors) are met.
- Political neutrality is maintained and endorsements avoided.
- Maintain consistent messaging that aligns with the Organization’s mission and 501(c)(3) requirements.

## 5. Post-Award Donor Retention Workflow

### 5.1 Step 1 – Verify Award and Conditions

**Responsible:**Development, with Finance and Programs

- Obtain and securely store:
- Signed grant agreement or award letter.
- Sponsorship or partnership contract.
- Email or letter documenting verbal commitments where no formal agreement is provided.
- Identify and document in the CRM:
- Award amount and payment schedule.
- Restricted vs. unrestricted status.
- Purpose, allowable costs, geographic or population focus.
- Reporting requirements (content, frequency, format, deadlines).
- Requirements for naming, logo usage, donor recognition, or approvals.
- Confidentiality clauses (e.g., whether terms or donor identity must remain private).
- Share a summary with relevant staff (Programs, Finance, Communications) and flag:
- Any unusual restrictions.
- Any media/branding requirements or limitations.
- Any political/lobbying prohibitions beyond standard 501(c)(3) rules.

### 5.2 Step 2 – Send Legally Compliant Acknowledgement

**Responsible:**Development

- Issue an acknowledgement letter or email (and tax receipt, if applicable) within**10 business days**of award notification or receipt of funds.
- Ensure the acknowledgement:
- States the**correct donor name**and entity (e.g., corporate foundation vs. operating company).
- States the**exact amount**(or notes “no goods or services were provided other than intangible religious benefits” or similar, if applicable to the Organization’s practice) and whether the gift is restricted or unrestricted.
- Does**not**overstate tax deductibility or provide tax advice. Use neutral language such as:
- “the Organization is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization. This letter serves as your acknowledgement for tax purposes. Please consult your tax advisor regarding the deductibility of this contribution.”
- Is consistent with any confidentiality or anonymity requests.

### 5.3 Step 3 – Configure Recognition and Stewardship Plan

**Responsible:**Development, with Communications and Programs

- Record donor recognition and communication preferences:
- Use of donor name (full name, organization name, logo variations).
- Anonymity, opt-out from public recognition, or specific channels to avoid.
- Frequency and type of updates (e.g., quarterly email, annual impact report, site visit).
- Create a donor-specific**Stewardship Plan**in the CRM that includes:
- Timelines for thank-you calls, emails, site visits, and reports.
- Key milestones (program start, mid-point, completion).
- Required approvals (ED, legal, board committee) for high-visibility recognition or unusual terms.

### 5.4 Step 4 – Implement Program Activities in Compliance with Restrictions

**Responsible:**Programs, Finance, Development

- Program staff implement funded work in alignment with:
- The**scope, budget, and timeline**in the grant agreement or gift documentation.
- the Organization’s mission, bylaws, nondiscrimination policies, and child-protection standards.
- Finance:
- Sets up appropriate accounts or cost centers to track restricted funds separately.
- Monitors spending to ensure compliance with donor restrictions and timelines.
- Development and Programs:
- Communicate early if any**material deviations**from the agreed scope, timeline, or budget are anticipated.
- Seek written donor approval before redirecting or reprogramming restricted funds.

### 5.5 Step 5 – Prepare and Approve Donor-Facing Communications

**Responsible:**Development (lead), with Communications, Programs, and ED as required

For every donor-facing stewardship communication (e.g., report, newsletter piece, social media post, press release, event remarks) that references a specific donor, grant, or funded project:

1. Drafting
- Development and/or Communications draft the content, ensuring:
- Accuracy of amounts, dates, project descriptions, and outcomes.
- Alignment with donor restrictions and recognition expectations.
- No overpromising of future outcomes or funding.
- No political endorsements, partisan content, or excessive lobbying language.
2. Program Review
- Program staff review for:
- Factual accuracy about program activities, beneficiaries, and impact.
- Appropriate anonymization or de-identification of children and families unless explicit, documented consent is on file.
- Realistic description of outcomes and limitations.
3. Finance Review (when applicable)
- For grant or financial reports, Finance reviews:
- Budget-to-actuals.
- Allocation of shared or indirect costs.
- Consistency with internal financial statements.
4. Compliance & Approval
- For all public-facing content that: Follow the Approval Workflow in Section 11.
- Uses donor names or logos.
- Highlights specific children/families.
- Announces large or multi-year grants or corporate partnerships.

## 6. Permission Checks for Donor Names, Logos, and Amounts

### 6.1 Donor Names and Public Recognition

- Obtain and document donor preferences:
- “Public recognition OK” / “Anonymous” / “Name only, no amount” / “Name and amount” / “Logo permitted.”
- If donor preferences are not documented:
- Default to**conservative recognition**: name without amount, no logo, and no detailed description of donor’s internal decision-making.
- Do not promise or grant naming rights (e.g., “the Organization Program X presented by Donor Y”) without:
- Internal review (Development, ED, and, where appropriate, Board or a Board committee).
- Consistency with the Organization’s mission, brand, and reputational standards.
- Written donor agreement describing the scope and term of naming.

### 6.2 Use of Logos and Brand Assets

- Never use a donor’s logo, brand tagline, or trademark without:
- Written permission from the donor (email or contract is sufficient if clear).
- Adhering to any brand guidelines they provide (colors, spacing, disclaimers).
- Maintain electronic copies of all logo-use permissions in the CRM or shared grant files.

### 6.3 Disclosure of Amounts

- Only disclose gift or grant amounts publicly if:
- The donor has explicitly authorized disclosure, or
- Disclosure is standard and expected in the donor’s sector (e.g., many large foundations publish grant amounts)**and**it is not prohibited in the agreement.
- When in doubt,**do not**publicly share the amount without donor confirmation.

#### Example – Names/Logos/Amounts

**Compliant “Do”**

- “We are grateful to the [Name] Foundation for supporting the Organization’s family mentorship programs.”
- “the Organization thanks [Corporate Donor] for their generous sponsorship of our 2026 Family Conference. Used with permission, [Corporate Donor]’s logo appears on event signage.”

**Noncompliant “Don’t”**

- “the Organization is fully funded by [Corporate Donor] and is now the exclusive charity partner for their products.”
- Posting a donor’s logo on the website banner without permission and tagging them in social media indicating a “strategic partnership” the donor has not approved.

## 7. Privacy, Media Releases, and Minors

### 7.1 General Privacy

- Treat all donor PII, and all PII regarding children and families, as**confidential**.
- Do not share names, contact info, or identifiable personal details externally without:
- A clear business need; and
- Appropriate consent or contractual permission.

### 7.2 Media Releases for Adults

- Obtain written media consent (or confirm existing consent) before using:
- Names and photos.
- Video or audio recordings.
- Quotes or personal stories that can reasonably identify individuals.
- Consent forms must explain:
- The types of media (print, web, social media).
- The purpose(s) of use.
- That consent can be withdrawn prospectively (not retroactively for materials already published, except as reasonably feasible).

### 7.3 Media Releases for Minors

- **Always**obtain written consent from a parent or legal guardian before:
- Publishing a minor’s name, likeness, or story.
- Sharing content that could identify a child (even if the face is not shown, but the context is uniquely identifying).
- When using minor-related stories without consent:
- Use composite or anonymized stories that**cannot**be traced back to specific children.
- Remove or alter location, age, and other identifying details.

### 7.4 Special Sensitivity for Children and Families Served

- Avoid language that:
- Stereotypes or stigmatizes the children or families served.
- Exploits personal hardship or trauma for fundraising (“poverty porn” or “inspiration porn” tropes).
- Highlight strengths, dignity, and agency of families and children.

#### Example – Privacy and Minors

**Compliant “Do”**

- “With permission from [Parent Name], we are grateful to share this story about their family’s experience with the Organization’s mentoring program. [Child’s first name]’s photo is used with parental consent.”
- “A family in our program (name withheld for privacy) shared that early access to sign language transformed their child’s confidence and school engagement.”

**Noncompliant “Don’t”**

- Posting a recognizable photo of a child at an Organization event without any signed media release, tagging the parent on social media.
- “Meet [Child’s full name], whose hearing loss left them completely isolated until the Organization rescued them,” when there is no consent and the language is exploitative.

## 8. Accuracy, No Overpromising, and Truthful Impact Reporting

- All donor communication must be:
- Factually accurate and verifiable.
- Clear about what has been achieved versus what is aspirational.
- Do**not**:
- Promise outcomes, staffing, or timelines that the Organization is not certain it can meet.
- Represent a grant as “renewed” or “permanent” unless it is actually multi-year and confirmed in writing.
- Claim that the Organization provides services or coverage in locations where it does not operate.

#### Example – Accuracy and Overpromising

**Compliant “Do”**

- “With your support, the Organization provided classes to 120 families this year and expanded mentoring for parents in three new regions.”
- “If additional funding is secured, the Organization plans to extend weekend family events to two more states in 2027.”

**Noncompliant “Don’t”**

- “Thanks to your gift, every child in the country will now have full access,” when the Organization cannot guarantee this.
- “We guarantee to double program enrollment next year,” when this is aspirational, not contractually or operationally certain.

## 9. Restricted Funds Use and 501(c)(3) Compliance

### 9.1 Restricted vs. Unrestricted Funds

- Restricted funds must be:
- Used**only**for the purposes, activities, and time periods specified by the donor.
- Tracked in separate accounts or cost centers.
- Unspent restricted funds:
- Must be handled according to the agreement (e.g., returned, reallocated with approval, extended grant period).
- Do not reprogram restricted funds (e.g., using program funds for general operating) without**written donor approval**.

### 9.2 501(c)(3) Lobbying and Political Activity

- the Organization may engage in**limited, nonpartisan advocacy**related to its mission, within IRS rules and applicable law.
- the Organization must**not**:
- Endorse or oppose any political candidate or party.
- Coordinate with campaigns or political action committees.
- Donor communications:
- Must not imply that a donor’s gift will be used to support or oppose a candidate or party.
- Should describe advocacy efforts in broad, mission-based terms (e.g., “supporting policies that improve access to services for the people we serve”) without partisan framing.

#### Example – Restricted Use & Political Compliance

**Compliant “Do”**

- “Your grant will support outreach to parents and caregivers, including informational materials and workshops about services for the families we serve.”
- “the Organization participates in nonpartisan policy education to advance equitable access to services for the people it serves, in accordance with its 501(c)(3) status.”

**Noncompliant “Don’t”**

- “Your donation will help the Organization defeat candidates who oppose our priorities.”
- Using restricted funds designated for family mentoring to cover the Organization’s unrelated general administrative expenses, without donor consent.

## 10. Endorsements, Attribution, and Disclaimer Language

### 10.1 Avoiding Endorsements

- the Organization must not imply:
- Product or service endorsements.
- That the Organization’s programs prefer one corporate sponsor’s goods or services over another.
- When acknowledging corporate partners, use neutral attribution, not promotional slogans.

### 10.2 Attribution Language

- Use clear, neutral attribution language such as:
- “Supported by…”
- “Funded in part by…”
- “Made possible through the generosity of…”
- Include disclaimers when required or appropriate, for example:
- “The views expressed in this publication do not necessarily reflect the views of [Donor].”

#### Example – Endorsements & Attribution

**Compliant “Do”**

- “This resource guide is funded in part by the [Corporate Foundation].”
- “the Organization thanks [Company] for sponsoring our 2026 National Family Conference.”

**Noncompliant “Don’t”**

- “the Organization recommends [Company’s] hearing aids as the best choice for all families.”
- “the Organization officially endorses [Brand] products for all the children we serve.”

## 11. Confidentiality of Grant Terms and Donor Information

- Treat grant agreements, donor contact information, and internal deliberations as**confidential**.
- Do not:
- Share exact contract clauses, proprietary donor evaluation criteria, or internal correspondence in public-facing materials.
- Circulate full grant agreements outside staff and board members who need access for their roles.
- When discussing grants externally:
- Refer to high-level terms (e.g., “a three-year grant to support family mentoring programs”) unless the donor has approved more detail.
- Omit or generalize sensitive financial or operational details if confidentiality clauses apply.

#### Example – Confidentiality

**Compliant “Do”**

- Internal staff memo summarizing key obligations from a grant agreement stored in a secure shared drive and CRM.
- External annual report: “the Organization received multi-year foundation support for its national mentoring program,” without disclosing confidential terms.

**Noncompliant “Don’t”**

- Posting a full, signed grant agreement (with financial schedules and proprietary donor terms) on the the Organization website.
- Sending a donor’s private email about internal strategy to another donor without permission.

## 12. Intellectual Property (IP) and Copyright for Assets

- Assume that:
- Donor logos, trademarks, and brand elements are protected IP owned by the donor.
- the Organization-created materials (e.g., curricula, toolkits, videos) are the Organization’s IP, unless a grant or contract explicitly states otherwise.
- Before granting any IP rights to a donor (e.g., joint ownership, license to use the Organization-created materials):
- Review the grant/contract language.
- Seek ED approval and legal review for complex or unusual IP clauses.
- In donor recognition materials:
- Use only images and content for which the Organization has rights (e.g., purchased stock, licensed images, or the Organization-owned photos with consent).

#### Example – IP and Copyright

**Compliant “Do”**

- “The curriculum developed under this grant remains the property of the Organization, but [Foundation] is granted a non-exclusive license to share it with their grantees as outlined in the grant agreement.”
- Using a corporate donor’s approved logo file as provided in their branding kit, within the agreed contexts.

**Noncompliant “Don’t”**

- Giving a donor exclusive ownership of the Organization’s core curriculum in a standard grant without ED and legal review.
- Downloading images from the internet without a license and using them in donor-facing reports.

## 13. Reporting Integrity and Record Retention

### 13.1 Reporting Integrity

- All programmatic and financial reports to donors must:
- Be accurate, complete, and timely.
- Match the Organization’s internal financial records and program data.
- If errors are discovered after a report is sent:
- Notify the ED and Finance.
- Correct the data and send a clarification to the donor as appropriate.

### 13.2 Record Retention

- Maintain secure electronic copies (and physical copies where required) of:
- Award letters and grant agreements.
- Donor acknowledgement letters and emails.
- Media releases and consent forms.
- Reports submitted to donors.
- Key correspondence documenting changes to scope, budgets, or deadlines.
- Store documents in:
- the Organization’s CRM, with appropriate privacy and access restrictions.
- the Organization’s designated secure file storage (e.g., shared drive or document management system) consistent with organizational retention schedules.
- Retain donor and grant records for at least the period required by:
- the Organization’s record retention policy.
- Applicable laws and regulations.
- Contractual obligations in the grant agreement.

#### Example – Reporting & Records

**Compliant “Do”**

- Uploading a signed grant agreement and each submitted report to the donor’s record in the CRM.
- Correcting a mis-reported number of program participants by sending an updated report and brief explanation to the donor.

**Noncompliant “Don’t”**

- Discarding donor correspondence and agreements once funds are received.
- Altering attendance numbers to make outcomes appear stronger without documentation.

## 14. Approval Workflow

The following approval workflow applies to**post-award donor communications, recognition, and reporting**. Use the most conservative path where there is doubt.

### 14.1 Routine Internal-Only Communications

Examples: Internal grant summary memo, internal stewardship plan, staff reminders.

- **Draft:**Development or Programs.
- **Review:**Manager in the relevant department.
- **Approval:**Department head (e.g., Director of Development, Director of Programs).

### 14.2 Standard Donor Acknowledgements and Updates (Private, Non-Public)

Examples: Thank-you email/letter to donor, routine progress email with non-sensitive content.

- **Draft:**Development.
- **Review:**Program Manager (if program content involved).
- **Approval:**Director of Development or designee.

### 14.3 Grant and Sponsorship Reports (Private, Non-Public)

- **Draft:**Development (narrative) and Finance (financials), with input from Programs.
- **Review:**
- Programs: program data, outcomes, stories.
- Finance: financial data and consistency with records.
- **Approval:**Director of Development**and**ED for:
- High-value grants (threshold set internally).
- Grants with complex or sensitive conditions.

### 14.4 Public Recognition, Media, and Marketing Content

Examples: Website posts, press releases, social media posts, event programs featuring donor names/logos, promotional videos.

- **Draft:**Communications/Marketing with input from Development.
- **Review:**
- Development: donor preferences, donor relations, and restrictions.
- Programs: accuracy of program content.
- **Approval:**
- Director of Development (all public donor content).
- ED approval required when content:
- Includes donor logos.
- Highlights minors or families.
- Announces major multi-year grants or corporate partnerships.
- Involves sensitive topics (e.g., policy advocacy, legal issues, reputational risk).

### 14.5 High-Risk or Unusual Agreements

Examples: Naming rights of programs or spaces, co-branded campaigns, complex IP arrangements, unusual restrictions, or political/lobbying sensitivity.

- **Draft:**Development, in consultation with ED.
- **Review:**
- ED.
- Legal counsel as needed.
- **Approval:**
- ED.
- Board or relevant Board committee for:
- Major naming rights.
- Arrangements that could impact the Organization’s mission, reputation, or 501(c)(3) status.

## 15. Training and Monitoring

- **Orientation:**All new staff and relevant contractors receive training on this SOP during onboarding.
- **Annual Refreshers:**Development, Programs, Communications, and Finance staff participate in yearly refresher sessions on:
- Donor stewardship best practices.
- 501(c)(3) compliance, privacy, and confidentiality.
- Updates to the Organization policies or legal requirements.
- **Monitoring and Audits:**
- Periodic internal reviews of donor files, reports, and communications to ensure consistency with this SOP.
- Corrective action and additional training as needed when issues are identified.

## 16. Escalation and Questions

Staff should promptly escalate to the**Director of Development**and**Executive Director**if:

- There is uncertainty about:
- Use of donor names, logos, or amounts.
- Privacy, confidentiality, or consent (especially for minors).
- IP ownership or licensing.
- Political or lobbying implications of a donor request.
- A donor requests:
- Changes to program scope or use of funds.
- Public statements that could be perceived as endorsements.
- Access to confidential or proprietary the Organization information.

Where necessary, the ED will consult legal counsel or the Board to ensure the Organization’s legal and ethical obligations are met.

## 17. Implementation and Review

- This SOP takes effect for all new post-award activities beginning**January 1, 2026**, and should be applied as feasible to existing grants and donor relationships.
- The ED and Director of Development will:
- Review this SOP at least every**two years**, or sooner if laws, regulations, or the Organization policies change.
- Recommend revisions to the Board or relevant committee for approval as needed.

By following this SOP, the Organization staff and leadership will maintain strong, trust-based relationships with donors while upholding the organization’s mission, protecting the privacy and dignity of the children and families it serves, and preserving the Organization’s 501(c)(3) compliance.
